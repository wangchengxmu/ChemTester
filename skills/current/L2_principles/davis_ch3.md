# Drugs from Nature, Then and Now

## Source
[Medicines by Design (Davis), Chapter 3](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Biological_Chemistry/Medicines_by_Design_(Davis)/03%3A_Drugs_from_Nature_Then_and_Now)

## Key Concepts

### Natural Products as Drug Sources
- Humans have sought medicines from nature (plants, animals, minerals) for thousands of years
- Ancient healers used willow bark (salicylic acid precursor), opium poppy (morphine), cinchona bark (quinine)
- ~50% of approved drugs derived from or inspired by natural products

### Ethnobotany and Drug Discovery
- Indigenous knowledge guides modern drug discovery
- Examples: rosy periwinkle (vinblastine, vincristine for leukemia), Pacific yew (paclitaxel/Taxol for cancer)
- Biodiversity hotspots are critical reservoirs for undiscovered bioactive compounds

### Marine Natural Products
- Ocean organisms produce unique secondary metabolites
- Examples: cone snail venom (ziconotide for chronic pain), sponges (antiviral/anticancer compounds)
- Marine pharmacology is a growing field due to chemical diversity

### Bioprospecting
- Systematic search for organisms producing valuable biochemicals
- Combines ecology, taxonomy, chemistry, and pharmacology
- Ethical and conservation concerns: benefit-sharing with source countries (Nagoya Protocol)

### Modern Natural Product Approaches
- Genome mining: identifying biosynthetic gene clusters in microbial genomes
- Combinatorial biosynthesis: engineering organisms to produce modified natural products
- Metagenomics: studying unculturable microorganisms for new compounds
