# ABCs of Pharmacology

## Source
[Medicines by Design (Davis), Chapter 1](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Biological_Chemistry/Medicines_by_Design_(Davis)/01%3A_ABCs_of_Pharmacology)

## Key Concepts

### What is Pharmacology?
- Pharmacology studies how the body reacts to medicines and how medicines affect the body
- Distinct from pharmacy (preparing and dispensing medicines)
- Broad discipline: discovery, development, testing of drug action
- Spans chemistry, genetics, cell biology, physiology, and engineering
- Sponsored by NIGMS (NIH)

### Drug-Body Interactions
- Pharmacodynamics: what the drug does to the body (receptor binding, signaling, therapeutic effects)
- Pharmacokinetics: what the body does to the drug (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion)
- Drug interactions: grapefruit juice affects cytochrome P450 enzymes → raises blood levels of some drugs
- Aspirin example: stomach burning due to direct irritation of gastric mucosa

### Key Terms
- **Receptor**: biological target molecule that a drug binds to
- **Agonist**: drug that activates a receptor
- **Antagonist**: drug that blocks a receptor
- **Efficacy**: maximum effect a drug can produce
- **Potency**: dose required to produce a given effect
- **Therapeutic index**: ratio of toxic dose to effective dose (TI = TD₅₀/ED₅₀)

### Drug Development Pipeline
- Basic research → preclinical testing → clinical trials (Phase I-III) → FDA approval → post-marketing surveillance

## Formulas
- Therapeutic Index: TI = TD₅₀ / ED₅₀
- Higher TI = safer drug (wider margin between effective and toxic doses)
