# Benchmark Organic Cyanohydrin and Strecker Sequence Disambiguation

Use this note for benchmark MCQs or short-answer prompts that describe a
carbonyl compound, cyanide or HCN, ammonia or an amine, and an aqueous workup
or hydrolysis step.

## General Sequence Rules

- Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition with HCN or cyanide to
  form cyanohydrins; this step installs a C-CN group at the former carbonyl
  carbon.
- Carbonyl compounds treated with ammonia or a primary amine and cyanide under
  Strecker-type conditions form alpha-aminonitriles.
- The aminonitrile is an intermediate. If the sequence explicitly includes
  water, aqueous acid/base workup, or hydrolysis after the HCN/ammonia stage,
  carry the nitrile through hydrolysis to the carboxylic acid. The overall
  Strecker product is an alpha-amino acid, not the isolated aminonitrile.
- If the question asks for the product before hydrolysis or says the
  aminonitrile is isolated, choose aminonitrile. If it asks for the product
  after the aqueous hydrolysis/workup step, choose amino acid.

## Option Disambiguation

- "Aminonitrile" corresponds to the pre-hydrolysis Strecker intermediate.
- "Amino acid" corresponds to the post-hydrolysis product.
- "Amide" can be a transient nitrile-hydrolysis intermediate, but it is not
  the normal final product of complete aqueous acid/base nitrile hydrolysis.
- "Amine" is a nitrile-reduction product; it requires reducing conditions such
  as LiAlH4 or catalytic hydrogenation, not HCN/ammonia/water alone.

## Provenance

- Local OpenStax organic aldehyde/ketone memory records HCN addition to
  carbonyls as cyanohydrin formation and notes that cyanohydrins can be
  hydrolyzed further.
- Local OpenStax organic carboxylic-acid/nitrile memory records acid- or
  base-catalyzed nitrile hydrolysis as conversion of R-CN to R-COOH through an
  amide intermediate.
