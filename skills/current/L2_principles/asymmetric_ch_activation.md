## Concept Overview

Asymmetric C-H activation uses chiral transition metal catalysts (Pd, Rh, Ir, Ru) to functionalize C-H bonds enantioselectively. This avoids pre-functionalization and is highly atom-economical.

### Key Strategies

| Strategy | Mechanism | Typical Metal | Typical ee |
|----------|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Directing group (DG) | Metal inserts into C-H directed by coordinating group | Pd(II), Rh(III), Ir(III) | 80-99% |
| Cyclopalladation | Chiral auxiliary on DG | Pd(II) | 90-99% |
| Transient directing groups | In-situ formed DG from substrate + chiral additive | Pd(II), Rh(III) | 70-95% |
| Chiral ligands on metal | External chiral ligand controls face selectivity | Pd, Rh, Ir | 70-95% |

### Directing Groups

Common DGs: pyridine, 8-aminoquinoline, pivaloyl, oxazoline, picolinamide. The DG coordinates the metal, bringing it proximal to a specific C-H bond.

### Chiral Catalysts

- Pd(II) with chiral phosphine-oxazoline (PHOX) ligands
- Rh(III) with chiral Cp* ligands (Cp^X)
- Ir(III) with chiral cyclopentadienyl ligands
