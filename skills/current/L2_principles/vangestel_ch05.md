# Population, Community and Ecosystem Ecotoxicology

## Source
[Environmental Toxicology (van Gestel et al.), Chapter 5](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Environmental_Chemistry/Environmental_Toxicology_(van_Gestel_et_al.)/05%3A_Population_Community_and_Ecosystem_Ecotoxicology)
Authors: Sylvia Moes, Kees van Gestel, & Gerco van Beek

## Key Concepts

### Population-Level Effects
- Population growth rate (r) and carrying capacity (K)
- Toxicants reduce reproduction, increase mortality, alter sex ratios
- Population models: Leslie matrices, logistic growth with toxicant effects
- Delayed effects: reduced recruitment, population decline after initial exposure

### Community-Level Effects
- Species diversity, evenness, richness as indicators
- Pollution-induced community tolerance (PICT)
- Keystone species vulnerability → cascading ecosystem effects
- Functional redundancy: some species can compensate for others' loss

### Ecosystem-Level Effects
- Energy flow and nutrient cycling disruption
- Primary productivity changes (eutrophication from nutrient pollution)
- Food web alterations (biomagnification through trophic levels)
- Ecosystem services: provision, regulation, cultural, supporting services

### Standardized Ecotoxicity Tests
- **Acute**: Daphnia magna 48h EC₅₀, fish 96h LC₅₀, algae 72h EC₅₀
- **Chronic**: reproduction tests (Daphnia, earthworms), growth inhibition
- **Bioassays**: Microtox (Vibrio fischeri luminescence), seed germination
- **Mesocosms/field studies**: intermediate between lab and real world

### Species Sensitivity Distribution (SSD)
- Compile toxicity data for multiple species
- Fit statistical distribution (usually log-normal or log-logistic)
- HC₅: hazardous concentration affecting 5% of species (protective level)
- Derive predicted no-effect concentration (PNEC) from SSD

## Formulas
- Logistic growth: dN/dt = rN(1 - N/K)
- HC₅ = 5th percentile of SSD
- PNEC = HC₅ / AF (assessment factor, typically 1-5)
