## Definition

The **lanthanide series** comprises the 14 elements from Cerium (Ce, Z=58) to Lutetium (Lu, Z=71), following Lanthanum (La, Z=57). These elements are characterized by progressive filling of the 4f orbitals and are collectively known as the **4f elements** or **rare earth metals** (together with scandium and yttrium).

## Core Concepts

### 1. Electron Configuration

After Lanthanum, the energy of the 4f subshell falls below that of the 5d subshell, so electrons begin filling the 4f orbital.

**General configuration:** [Xe] 4f^n 5d^0-1 6s^2

| Element | Symbol | Idealized Config | Observed Config |
|---------|--------|------------------|-----------------|
| La | La | 5d¹6s² | 5d¹6s² |
| Ce | Ce | 4f¹5d¹6s² | 4f¹5d¹6s² |
| Pr | Pr | 4f²5d¹6s² | 4f³6s² |
| Nd | Nd | 4f³5d¹6s² | 4f⁴6s² |
| Pm | Pm | 4f⁴5d¹6s² | 4f⁵6s² |
| Sm | Sm | 4f⁵5d¹6s² | 4f⁶6s² |
| Eu | Eu | 4f⁶5d¹6s² | 4f⁷6s² |
| Gd | Gd | 4f⁷5d¹6s² | 4f⁷5d¹6s² |
| Tb | Tb | 4f⁸5d¹6s² | 4f⁹6s² |
| Dy | Dy | 4f⁹5d¹6s² | 4f¹⁰6s² |
| Ho | Ho | 4f¹⁰5d¹6s² | 4f¹¹6s² |
| Er | Er | 4f¹¹5d¹6s² | 4f¹²6s² |
| Tm | Tm | 4f¹²5d¹6s² | 4f¹³6s² |
| Yb | Yb | 4f¹³5d¹6s² | 4f¹⁴6s² |
| Lu | Lu | 4f¹⁴5d¹6s² | 4f¹⁴5d¹6s² |

**Key observations:**
- Gd (4f⁷) and Lu (4f¹⁴5d¹) have half-filled and fully-filled f-subshell stability
- Deviations from idealized configurations due to f-d energy proximity

### 2. Oxidation States

**Dominant oxidation state:** +3 (most stable and common for all lanthanides)

**Exceptions:**
- **Ce⁴⁺** (stable, strong oxidant, used in redox chemistry)
- **Eu²⁺** (stable, reducing agent)
- **Yb²⁺** (stable under certain conditions)
- **Tb⁴⁺** (less common, oxidizing)
- **Pr⁴⁺** (rare, in oxides)

**Why +3 dominates:**
- Removal of two 6s and one 5d/4f electron gives noble gas configuration
- Further ionization requires very high energy (removing from 4f)

### 3. Physical Properties

| Property | Trend |
|----------|-------|
| **Atomic radius** | Decreases from La to Lu (lanthanide contraction) |
| **Ionic radius (Ln³⁺)** | Decreases from 106.1 pm (La³⁺) to 84.8 pm (Lu³⁺) |
| **Melting point** | Generally increases across series |
| **Density** | Increases across series |
| **Ionization energy** | Increases across series |

### 4. Chemical Characteristics

**General properties:**
- Silvery-white metals, tarnish quickly in air
- React slowly with cold water, faster when heated
- Dissolve readily in acids
- Oxidize rapidly in moist air
- Electropositive nature (similar to Group 2 metals)
- Form primarily ionic compounds

**Coordination chemistry:**
- High coordination numbers (typically 8-9, up to 12)
- Ionic bonding dominates (little covalent character)
- Little ligand field stabilization energy (LFSE)
- Rapid ligand exchange
- Preference for hard donor atoms (O, F)

### 5. Basicity Series

Basicity decreases across the series (ease of losing electrons decreases):

La³⁺ > Ce³⁺ > Pr³⁺ > Nd³⁺ > Pm³⁺ > Sm³⁺ > Eu³⁺ > Gd³⁺ > Tb³⁺ > Dy³⁺ > Ho³⁺ > Er³⁺ > Tm³⁺ > Yb³⁺ > Lu³⁺

**Implication:** Lighter lanthanides form more basic hydroxides and are less readily complexed.

### 6. Magnetic Properties

**Diamagnetic (no unpaired electrons):**
- La³⁺ (4f⁰)
- Lu³⁺ (4f¹⁴)
- Yb²⁺ (4f¹⁴)
- Ce⁴⁺ (4f⁰)

**Paramagnetic (unpaired electrons):** All other lanthanide ions

**Magnetic moments:**
- Calculated using spin-orbit coupling (unlike d-block where spin-only formula applies)
- Gd³⁺ (4f⁷) has maximum moment: 7.94 μB
- Experimental values agree well with Russell-Saunders coupling theory

### 7. Spectroscopic Properties and Color

**Electronic transitions:**
- f-f transitions are Laporte-forbidden (weak absorption)
- Sharp line-like spectra (shielded 4f orbitals)
- Weak crystal field effects
- Colors arise from f-f transitions

| Ion | Unpaired e⁻ | Color (aq) |
|-----|-------------|------------|
| La³⁺ | 0 | Colorless |
| Ce³⁺ | 1 | Colorless |
| Pr³⁺ | 2 | Green |
| Nd³⁺ | 3 | Reddish |
| Pm³⁺ | 4 | Pink/yellow |
| Sm³⁺ | 5 | Yellow |
| Eu³⁺ | 6 | Pale pink |
| Gd³⁺ | 7 | Colorless |
| Tb³⁺ | 6 | Pale pink |
| Dy³⁺ | 5 | Yellow |
| Ho³⁺ | 4 | Pink/yellow |
| Er³⁺ | 3 | Reddish |
| Tm³⁺ | 2 | Green |
| Yb³⁺ | 1 | Colorless |
| Lu³⁺ | 0 | Colorless |

### 8. Luminescence

**Key property:** Many lanthanide ions exhibit sharp, line-like emission due to f-f transitions.

**Important luminescent ions:**
- **Eu³⁺** - Red emission (~615 nm)
- **Tb³⁺** - Green emission (~545 nm)
- **Dy³⁺** - Yellow emission
- **Sm³⁺** - Orange emission
- **Ce³⁺** - UV emission (5d→4f transition)

**Applications:**
- Phosphors in displays and lighting
- Fluorescent lamps
- LED lighting
- Anti-counterfeiting inks
- Biomedical imaging (lanthanide complexes)

**Energy transfer:** Ligand-to-metal energy transfer (antenna effect) enhances luminescence efficiency.

## Key Formulas

### Ionic Radius (Shannon radii for CN=6)
- La³⁺: 103.2 pm
- Ce³⁺: 101 pm
- Pr³⁺: 99 pm
- Nd³⁺: 98.3 pm
- ...decreasing to...
- Lu³⁺: 86.1 pm

### Lanthanide Contraction Formula
The decrease in ionic radius across the series is approximately:
- **~15 pm total decrease** from La³⁺ to Lu³⁺
- Average decrease: ~1 pm per element

## Common Lanthanide Compounds

### Oxides
- Formula: Ln₂O₃ (most common)
- Exceptions: CeO₂, Pr₆O₁₁, Tb₄O₇
- Basic oxides, absorb CO₂ from air

### Halides
- LnX₃ (most common, X = F, Cl, Br, I)
- LnF₃ is insoluble (used in separation)
- CeF₄, TbF₄ exist (M⁴⁺ oxidation state)

### Hydroxides
- Ln(OH)₃ - gelatinous precipitates
- Basicity decreases across series
- Precipitate at lower pH for heavier lanthanides

### Coordination Complexes
- High coordination numbers (8-10 typical)
- Common ligands: EDTA, DTPA, β-diketonates, crown ethers
- Organometallics: Cp₃Ln, (C₅H₅)₃Ln

## Occurrence and Extraction

**Main ores:**
1. **Monazite** - phosphate mineral, lighter lanthanides
2. **Bastnäsite** - fluoride-carbonate, lighter lanthanides
3. **Xenotime** - phosphate, heavier lanthanides

**Extraction methods:**
1. Solvent extraction (liquid-liquid extraction)
2. Ion exchange chromatography
3. Selective precipitation

**Separation challenge:** Similar chemical properties require multiple extraction stages.

## Applications

1. **Catalysis:** Ce in automotive catalysts, La in petroleum cracking
2. **Magnets:** Nd₂Fe₁₄B (neodymium magnets), SmCo₅
3. **Phosphors:** Eu, Tb in displays and lighting
4. **Glass/ceramics:** Ce for UV absorption, Nd for coloring
5. **Metallurgy:** La, Ce for grain refinement in steel
6. **Nuclear:** Eu, Gd as neutron absorbers
