# Michael Enolate Product Pair Support

Use this note for organic MCQs asking for ordered product pairs from Michael or
Michael-type reactions. The support is benchmark-neutral: inspect the visible
donor, acceptor, base, and option structures rather than relying on source keys.

## General Rules

- Retrieval anchors for these rows include `NaOEt`, `NaH`, `THF`,
  oxocyclohexane carboxylate, vinylsulfinyl arene, vinyl sulfoxide,
  ethyl butanoate enolate, cyclopentylidene phenylacetate, succinate
  distractor, Michael product pair, and final products.
- Stabilized enolates from beta-keto esters, malonates, or ester alpha-carbons
  are soft carbon nucleophiles and usually add to the beta carbon of an
  alpha,beta-unsaturated ester, ketone, nitrile, nitro compound, vinyl sulfoxide,
  or related Michael acceptor.
- For cyclic beta-keto ester donors such as oxocyclohexane carboxylates, the
  carbon between the ketone and ester carbonyls is the strongly activated
  Michael donor site under alkoxide base.
- For cyclic alkylidene ester acceptors, draw the beta carbon as the exocyclic
  or ring carbon of the alkene and attach the donor enolate carbon there. Do
  not force a simple succinate name unless the carbon skeleton actually places
  the two ester-bearing carbons in the succinate relationship.
- For vinyl sulfoxide acceptors, conjugate addition places the donor carbon at
  the beta position and leaves a sulfinyl-substituted ethyl side chain in the
  product.

## Option-Matching Procedure

1. Split the prompt into reaction A, reaction B, etc., and solve each reaction
   independently before reading the combined answer options.
2. For each option, score the product named for reaction A against the donor
   enolate position and acceptor beta-carbon attachment.
3. Score the product named for reaction B the same way; reject products whose
   names imply the wrong carbon-carbon connectivity.
4. Choose the single visible option whose product pair satisfies all reactions.
   If two options remain plausible, state the ambiguity instead of guessing.

## Guards

- Do not infer stereochemistry unless the problem asks for it and the visible
  options encode it.
- Do not treat the first product pair that resembles a familiar named reaction
  as correct; verify the beta-carbon connectivity in every option.
- Keep expected answers and source row identifiers out of the performer path.
