# Organic Reactivity: Aryl Ether Cationic Cyclization Under HX

Use this note when an aryl ether bearing an alkene side chain is treated with a
strong hydrogen halide such as HBr, hydrogen bromide, or HI and the question asks
for possible products, TLC product spots, chromanes, dihydrobenzofurans, or
simple addition versus intramolecular cyclization.

## Recognition

- An aryl ether with a pendant alkene can be protonated by HX to form a
  carbocation-like electrophile on the side chain.
- Prenyl, dimethylallyl, homoallyl, and 2,2-dimethylbut-3-en-1-yl aryl ethers
  are common visible motifs for this pattern.
- The anisole-like aromatic ring is activated by the alkoxy substituent and can
  attack intramolecularly when the geometry permits.
- Intramolecular electrophilic aromatic substitution can close five- or six-
  membered oxygen-containing rings.
- Common product families include benzofuran/dihydrobenzofuran and
  chromane/chroman frameworks, depending on which tether position closes.
- Names such as trimethylchromane, isopropyl-methyl dihydrobenzofuran, chroman,
  and dihydrobenzofuran point to cyclic aryl ether products rather than simple
  open-chain bromide addition.

## Procedure

1. Identify whether the substrate is an alkene-containing aryl ether rather than
   a simple alkyl aryl ether with no internal electrophile.
2. Under HX, compare simple Markovnikov halide addition with formation of a more
   stable carbocation that can be trapped intramolecularly by the activated arene.
3. If two TLC product spots are reported, consider regioisomeric ring closures or
   competing five- and six-membered cyclic ether products.
4. Check that proposed products preserve the aryl oxygen in a cyclic ether
   framework; options showing only open-chain bromo ethers or free phenols may be
   distractors unless the prompt specifically asks for cleavage.

## Guards

- Do not force cyclization for every aryl ether plus HBr problem; require a
  pendant alkene or another internal electrophile and an activated arene.
- Do not rely on exact substrate names or benchmark option order. Use the
  electrophile/arene/tether pattern.
- Treat TLC evidence as evidence for multiple products, not as proof of any
  specific structure by itself.
