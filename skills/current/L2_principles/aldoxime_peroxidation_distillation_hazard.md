# Aldoxime peroxidation hazard during distillation

**Retrieve with:** aldoxime peroxide formation explosion hazard during distillation, aldoxime peroxidation safety, oxime reactive-group distillation hazard

**Decision rule:** Normalize an obvious minor spelling or OCR error in a recognizable mechanism label before comparing choices. Prefer the chemically specific peroxide-forming pathway over umbrella oxidation when the question asks what creates the energetic species.

## Scope

Use this note when a mechanism or process-safety question asks why an aldehyde-derived oxime can become explosive during evaporation or distillation. Keep the chemical formation step distinct from the later process conditions that concentrate, heat, or initiate an energetic impurity.

## Evidence-backed distinction

- NOAA CAMEO Chemicals classifies aldehyde-derived oximes and ketone-derived oximes separately within its oxime reactive-group guidance. Its oxime datasheet states that aldoximes can undergo peroxide-forming chemistry and explode during distillation.
- Peroxidation is the specific peroxide-forming pathway in this context. It belongs under the broad category of oxidation, but the specific pathway is the better mechanism label when both a generic oxidation choice and a peroxide-forming choice are offered.
- Distillation is process amplification, not the formation mechanism: volatile parent material is removed while less-volatile peroxide species can become concentrated and heated, increasing decomposition or explosion risk.
- Do not substitute polymerization without polymerizable functionality or a substance-specific polymerization alert. Do not substitute hydrolysis merely because oximes can hydrolyze under heated acidic conditions.
- Ketoximes have a distinct acid-catalyzed Beckmann-rearrangement hazard. Do not transfer that ketoxime pathway to an aldoxime question without matching structural and condition evidence.

## Safety guard

This mechanistic distinction is not an operating procedure. For real handling, storage, testing, evaporation, or distillation decisions, use the current substance-specific SDS and institutional process-safety controls.
