# X-Ray XRD BCC FCC Lattice Systematic Absence Support

Use this note when X-ray diffraction data are used to distinguish simple cubic, body-centred cubic, and face-centred cubic metals.

- Retrieval anchors: unknown metallic sample, X-ray diffraction, XRD, BCC, FCC, simple cubic, sin2 theta, sin^2 theta, Cu K-alpha, Mo K-alpha, peak count, large unit cell.
- Bragg peaks are indexed by allowed lattice planes. Compare patterns using sin^2(theta) ratios, peak counts in a fixed 2 theta window, and systematic absences.
- BCC lattices allow reflections with h + k + l even and systematically miss h + k + l odd reflections.
- FCC lattices allow reflections whose h, k, and l are all even or all odd; mixed parity reflections are systematically absent.
- If the prompt says the observed intensities do not follow BCC systematic absences, do not choose a pure BCC structure.
- With a shorter X-ray wavelength, more reciprocal-lattice points can satisfy Bragg's law within the same 2 theta window. More observed peaks with shorter wavelength can indicate a larger unit cell rather than a smaller one.
- For a metallic sample with many peaks, no BCC absence pattern, and evidence consistent with FCC indexing, prefer an FCC conclusion over simple cubic or BCC distractors.
