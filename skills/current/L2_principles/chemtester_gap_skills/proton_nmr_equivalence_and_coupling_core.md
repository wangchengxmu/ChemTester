# Proton NMR Equivalence and Coupling Core

**Retrieve with:** proton equivalence substitution test, diastereotopic
methylene coupling, geminal vicinal ABX

## Equivalence

1. Fix connectivity, stereochemistry, symmetry, conformation, and the relevant
   NMR timescale before counting proton environments.
2. Apply the substitution test to same-carbon hydrogens: replacing each in turn
   by the same test group gives identical structures for homotopic nuclei,
   enantiomers for enantiotopic nuclei, and diastereomers for diastereotopic
   nuclei.
3. In an achiral medium, enantiotopic nuclei normally share a chemical shift;
   diastereotopic nuclei can differ. Exchange or accidental overlap can still
   hide a real non-equivalence.

## Coupling

1. Treat chemically nonequivalent hydrogens as separate spins.
2. List coupling partners and coupling constants by pathway: geminal
   two-bond, vicinal three-bond, then justified longer-range couplings.
3. Compose first-order splittings successively only for resolved unequal
   coupling sets. One geminal and one vicinal coupling commonly gives a
   doublet of doublets for each resolved methylene proton.
4. Use AB or ABX language and expect second-order distortion when chemical
   shift separation is comparable to the relevant coupling constant.

## Guards

- A nearby stereogenic center is a trigger to test topicity, not proof by
  itself.
- Do not collapse geminal and vicinal partners into one n+1 count.
- Do not infer equivalence from one overlapped peak.
- Map every reported splitting and J value to a defensible coupling partner.
