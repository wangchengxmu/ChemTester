# Purification feasibility by speciation, phase, and unit-operation audit

**Retrieve with:** purification oxidation-state ledger, separation phase audit, leaching versus extraction, speciation and unit operations

**Use when:** A purification or separation claim depends on whether reagents cause redox, complexation, dissolution, precipitation, or transfer between phases.

## Procedure

1. List target and impurity species before and after each stated condition, including phases and oxidation states.
2. Classify each transformation independently as redox, acid-base reaction, complexation, dissolution or leaching, precipitation, or phase transfer.
3. Build an operation ledger distinguishing reaction, leaching, liquid-liquid extraction, evaporation, and filtration; count only operations stated or required by phase evidence.
4. Track target, impurity, and byproducts through every phase and verify that the stated recovery operation actually separates them.
5. Evaluate every claim independently, preserve separately verified quantitative conclusions, and map the complete truth set only at the end.

## Guards

- An oxidizing or reducing medium does not prove that every dissolved species changes oxidation state.
- Halide formation or complexation is not redox unless the central atom's oxidation state changes.
- Do not infer liquid-liquid extraction from dissolution, leaching, precipitation, or filtration.
- A chemical transformation alone does not prove purification without a valid phase-separation and recovery step.
