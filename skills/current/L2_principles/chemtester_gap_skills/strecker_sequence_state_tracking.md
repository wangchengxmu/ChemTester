# Strecker sequence intermediate-to-product tracking

**Retrieve with:** Strecker aminonitrile hydrolysis, carbonyl cyanide ammonia amino acid, nitrile workup final product

**Use when:** A carbonyl reaction sequence contains cyanide or HCN, ammonia or an amine, and a later aqueous hydrolysis or workup, so the requested product may lie beyond an aminonitrile intermediate.

## Procedure

1. Track the carbonyl carbon through imine or iminium formation and cyanide addition to the alpha-aminonitrile; preserve the original carbon substituents on that center.
2. Treat the aminonitrile as the isolated product only when the sequence stops before hydrolysis or explicitly isolates that intermediate.
3. When complete aqueous acid or base hydrolysis follows, convert the nitrile carbon to a carboxylic acid or carboxylate through the amide stage while retaining the alpha amino substituent, giving the corresponding alpha-amino-acid framework after workup.
4. Treat an explicitly named final hydrolysis or aqueous hydrolysis workup as a product-forming operation even when the cyanide and ammonia additions are listed separately or in an unconventional operational order.
5. Distinguish nitrile hydrolysis from nitrile reduction: an amide can be an intermediate in hydrolysis, whereas a primary amine requires reducing conditions.

## Guards

- Do not stop at the first recognizable intermediate when later reagents transform it.
- Do not ignore a final step explicitly described as nitrile hydrolysis or hydrolysis workup.
- Do not call water alone a complete nitrile hydrolysis unless the wording establishes an aqueous hydrolysis or workup step.
- Do not confuse the carboxamide intermediate with the final product of complete hydrolysis.
- Do not infer nitrile reduction without a reducing reagent.
