# Addition-mechanism selectivity disambiguation

**Retrieve with:** hydroboration concerted transition state, stereospecific versus regioselective, conjugated diene 1,2 1,4 addition, carbocation rearrangement alkene addition

**Use when:** An organic addition question asks why one product is observed or compares concertedness, stereospecificity, syn/anti addition, rearrangement, regiochemistry, and temperature effects.

## Procedure

1. List the alternatives that could multiply products: constitutional or regioisomers, rearranged skeletons, stereoisomers, and conformers; identify which type the observation excludes.
2. Classify the mechanism as concerted, carbocation-mediated, radical, or cyclic-intermediate, then determine whether rearrangement or competing 1,2/1,4 pathways are available.
3. For hydroboration, use the four-center concerted addition to exclude a free carbocation and ordinary hydride or alkyl shifts; treat syn addition and chiral-reagent facial control as separate consequences.
4. Test each explanation for sufficiency: stereospecific or syn behavior constrains relative stereochemistry but does not by itself eliminate regioisomers, constitutional products, or enantiomeric alternatives.
5. Use temperature diagnostically: changing product ratios supports competing kinetic and thermodynamic pathways, whereas invariance is an observation that still needs a mechanistic cause.

## Guards

- Do not infer a unique product from stereospecific, stereoselective, or syn without checking connectivity and regiochemistry.
- Do not infer uniqueness from concertedness alone; verify symmetry, facial selectivity, and other concerted pathways.
- Do not use an unrelated pericyclic-reaction retrieval hit as support for alkene hydroboration.
