# Living block-copolymer architecture and terminal-block audit

**Retrieve with:** living ABA block copolymer synthesis, sequential monomer addition triblock, bifunctional initiator symmetric block polymer, living diblock coupling

**Use when:** A polymer-synthesis question asks which routes can construct a symmetric A-B-A or related multiblock architecture with matching terminal blocks or controlled chain ends.

## Procedure

1. Translate the target into a block sequence and identify whether the wording requires matching terminal blocks, identical chemical end functionalities, or both.
2. For sequential monomer addition, require persistent living or controlled chain ends; an A feed followed by B and then A can form A-B-A when each new feed extends the surviving chains.
3. For a bifunctional initiator, map growth outward from the center and choose the feed sequence so both chain directions terminate in the same outer block.
4. For coupling, orient two compatible living diblocks so their reactive inner ends join; merge adjacent identical inner blocks when assigning the final architecture.
5. Reject routes whose uncontrolled initiation, transfer, or termination prevents reliable block order or terminal symmetry.

## Guards

- Sequential feeding does not produce a defined multiblock architecture without living or otherwise persistent chain-end control.
- Matching terminal A blocks do not by themselves prove identical molecular end-group functionality.
- A bifunctional initiator gives symmetric growth only when both initiating sites and propagation conditions are comparable.
- Coupling is valid only when the reactive chain ends and block orientation produce the intended sequence.
