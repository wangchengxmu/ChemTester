# Nucleophile-initiated strained-ring propagation versus quenched addition

**Retrieve with:** epoxide nucleophilic ring opening polymerization, strained heterocycle nucleophile initiator, alkoxide propagation versus workup, reactivity compatibility polymerization initiation

**Use when:** A qualitative reactivity or compatibility question pairs a nucleophile or base with an epoxide or another strained heterocyclic monomer and asks whether the outcome is initiation, propagation, or an isolated ring-opened product.

## Procedure

1. Classify the context before naming a product: distinguish a single-substrate synthesis with explicit quench or workup from a monomer, bulk-mixture, compatibility, or hazard setting.
2. Draw the first elementary step and retain its charge: nucleophilic ring opening normally gives an anionic heteroatom terminus, not a neutral product unless a proton source is supplied.
3. When more strained-ring monomer is available and no terminating proton source is stated, test one propagation step in which the anionic terminus attacks another monomer; classify the original reagent as an initiator if chain growth is viable.
4. Choose a discrete ring-opened addition product only when stoichiometry and an explicit proton donor, quench, or workup terminate the active center; when conditions are underspecified, use the question framing and option semantics to distinguish synthetic-product from compatibility intent.

## Guards

- Do not invent protonation, hydrolysis, or workup conditions that the prompt does not supply.
- Do not stop at the first ring-opened intermediate before testing whether its charged terminus can attack another monomer.
- Do not claim polymerization for every nucleophile-ring pair; require a polymerizable strained monomer, a viable propagating center, and no dominant termination condition.
