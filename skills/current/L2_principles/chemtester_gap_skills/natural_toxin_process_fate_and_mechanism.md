# Natural toxin process-fate and mechanism audit

**Retrieve with:** plant protein toxin oil extraction, water-soluble protein phase partition, heated extraction protein inactivation, ribosome-inactivating protein mechanism

**Use when:** A qualitative or multi-select toxicology question asks whether a natural protein toxin remains in a processed oil, is inactivated during processing, or acts through a proposed cellular target.

## Procedure

1. Classify the toxin chemically: water-soluble proteins generally follow aqueous or solid residue rather than a neutral lipid fraction.
2. Trace pressing, phase separation, refining, heating, and waste streams separately; product absence may reflect partitioning, removal, thermal inactivation, or several mechanisms.
3. Evaluate thermal claims against process conditions: sufficiently hot and long treatment can denature protein toxins, whereas cold pressing alone does not establish inactivation.
4. Check the molecular target independently: ribosome-inactivating proteins enzymatically damage rRNA and halt protein synthesis rather than primarily disrupting ion channels.
5. Match every statement to conventional versus explicitly cold processing, evaluate selections independently, and return the complete requested set.

## Guards

- Do not infer destruction solely from absence in a product; distinguish phase partitioning, physical removal, and inactivation.
- Do not apply heated-process conclusions to explicitly cold processing.
- For conventional commercial processing, do not reject an authoritative heat-inactivation claim merely because phase partitioning is also operative.
- Do not confuse a protein toxin with lipid-derived constituents from the same biological source.
