# Mercury toxicology by species, latency, and endpoint type

**Retrieve with:** mercury species toxic effects, organic mercury delayed neurotoxicity, mercurialentis exposure biomarker

**Use when:** A mercury toxicology question asks which clinical effect best matches an exposure form, route, duration, latency, or endpoint type.

## Procedure

1. Classify mercury form, exposure route, duration, latency, and population from the stem and options.
2. Separate patient symptoms and adverse effects from examination signs, exposure biomarkers, renal findings, and acute local injury.
3. Map organic mercury primarily to delayed sensory and cerebellar central-nervous-system toxicity; map chronic elemental or inorganic exposure to tremor, behavioral change, oral inflammation, and renal effects; map ingested inorganic salts to acute corrosive and renal injury.
4. Rank candidates using the complete exposure pattern and requested endpoint type, then return only the requested answer representation.

## Guards

- Do not treat a memorable eponym as the key symptom without checking whether it is only an exposure marker.
- Mercurialentis is lens-capsule discoloration rather than lens opacity and can occur as an exposure biomarker without adverse ocular effects.
- When mercury species is unstated, infer it only from the joint latency, route, organ-system, and option clues.
