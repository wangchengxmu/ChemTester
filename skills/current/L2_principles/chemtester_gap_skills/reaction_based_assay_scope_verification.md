# Verify reaction-based assay applicability before transferring stoichiometry

**Retrieve with:** reaction assay applicability, quantitative phenol iodination, fixed stoichiometry derivatization, iodometric analog assay

**Use when:** A titrimetric or derivatization assay asks whether a structurally related analyte can be measured by the same reaction and conditions.

## Procedure

1. Identify the analytical reaction, measured reagent equivalents, medium, reaction time, and proposed product for each analyte independently.
2. Require rapid and near-complete conversion, adequate selectivity, and a reproducible fixed reagent-to-analyte stoichiometry; mere qualitative reaction feasibility is insufficient.
3. For phenolic iodination, map free ring hydrogens and hydroxyl activation, then verify substitution extent: highly activated polyhydroxy rings may undergo exhaustive substitution, whereas monohydric phenol can give condition-dependent mono-, di-, and tri-substituted mixtures.
4. Only after assay applicability is established should its equivalent factor enter the back-titration balance; evaluate qualitative scope claims separately from otherwise correct arithmetic.

## Guards

- Qualitative reactivity does not establish quantitative assayability.
- Do not transfer stoichiometric equivalents between analogs without product-distribution evidence.
- Do not let correct titration arithmetic validate a separate method-scope claim.
- Do not generalize pH, time, or substitution extent across phenolic substrates.
