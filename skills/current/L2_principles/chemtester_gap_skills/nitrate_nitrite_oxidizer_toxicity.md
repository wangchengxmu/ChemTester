# Nitrate/nitrite chemistry and dual-mechanism toxicity

**Retrieve with:** nitrite toxicity symptoms, methemoglobinemia severity, nitrite vasodilation tachycardia

**Use when:** A nitrate or nitrite question asks about chemical role, toxicity mechanism, or a symptom pattern that may combine impaired oxygen transport with cardiovascular effects.

## Procedure

1. Distinguish direct nitrite exposure from nitrate exposure that first requires biological conversion to nitrite.
2. Model nitrite toxicity with both hemoglobin oxidation to methemoglobin and vascular smooth-muscle relaxation rather than using either mechanism alone.
3. Map methemoglobinemia to impaired oxygen delivery and cyanosis, and map vasodilation to reduced blood pressure with compensatory tachycardia; allow these findings to overlap at low exposure.
4. Compare each candidate symptom bundle against the combined mechanism and stated severity, using toxicant-specific evidence instead of equating a generic severity label with an asymptomatic stage.

## Guards

- Do not collapse nitrite intoxication into a methemoglobin-percentage table alone.
- Do not assume mild intoxication means symptomless unless exposure or clinical data establish that stage.
- Do not transfer direct-nitrite effects unchanged to nitrate without checking route and conversion context.
