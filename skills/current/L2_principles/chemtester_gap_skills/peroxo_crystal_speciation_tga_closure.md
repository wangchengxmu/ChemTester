# Peroxo-crystal speciation and TGA closure

**Retrieve with:** peroxocarbonate crystal speciation, peroxide solvate versus peroxoanion, TGA residue formula closure, crystal component multiplicity

**Use when:** A crystalline inorganic peroxide or peroxosalt identity, component ratio, or statement set must be inferred from preparation, ionic and neutral species, and thermogravimetric mass loss.

## Procedure

1. Write charged and neutral crystal components separately; distinguish hydrogen-monoperoxocarbonate such as HCO4-, peroxodicarbonate C2O6^2-, and neutral H2O2 of crystallization before collapsing to an empirical formula.
2. Check whether constitutional candidates share aggregate atom counts on a common formula-unit basis; if they do, TGA cannot determine their ion and guest multiplicities, so use preparation and crystallographic identity evidence for speciation.
3. For each viable constitution, compute the retained-residue fraction on a stoichiometrically matched basis and reject candidates inconsistent with the measured mass loss.
4. Balance all solid and volatile products with coprime coefficients, then derive component ratios and reaction coefficients from the accepted constitutional formula rather than its empirical composition.
5. Evaluate symmetry and bond-length claims independently from the actual conformation or reliable structural data before combining statement results and mapping the requested answer format.

## Guards

- One type of cation, anion, or neutral species states chemical identity, not stoichiometric count.
- Elemental and TGA agreement does not prove constitutional speciation when candidates are isocompositional.
- Do not infer symmetry elements or peroxo-bond ordering from the word peroxide alone.
- Match formula-unit and residue coefficients before comparing molar masses.
