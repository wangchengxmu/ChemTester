# Coordination-complex composition, topology, and stereochemical symmetry audit

**Retrieve with:** polynuclear coordination stereochemistry, metal site equivalence symmetry, Delta Lambda octahedral chirality, coordination ring size census, bridging ligand balance

**Use when:** A mono- or polynuclear coordination-complex problem asks about composition or reaction balance, donor geometry or orbital types, chelate-ring sizes, local Δ/Λ configurations, metal-site equivalence, molecular symmetry, or stability claims.

## Procedure

1. Build the full coordination graph, including ligand formal charges and denticity, released ligands and protons, and counterions; balance atoms and charge before comparing coefficient sums.
2. For each metal, list donor atoms, coordination number, geometry, and oxidation or d-electron count; if hybridization language is requested, count angular-momentum orbital types rather than individual orbitals.
3. Enumerate independent organic cycles and metallacycles and record the atom count of each; distinguish ring size from ring identity and follow the stated counting scope before collapsing categories.
4. Assign local Δ/Λ descriptors, then test site equivalence using a full-structure symmetry operation that maps one metal and every labeled donor path onto the other; proper operations preserve local handedness, whereas inversion or reflection reverses it.
5. Toggle every stereogenic element that the question includes or excludes, audit each statement independently, and only then map the true-statement set to the visible choices.

## Guards

- Identical local Δ/Λ labels do not prove metal-site equivalence; require a valid full-molecule symmetry mapping.
- A heterochiral descriptor pair does not by itself prove centrosymmetry or exceptional stability; the bridge, donor labels, conformation, and all included stereogenic centers must also map.
- Do not conflate chelate-ring size, aromatic-ring size, and connectivity when counting ring types; state the counting convention.
- Reject an atom-balanced synthesis if charge, protons, displaced ligands, or counterions are inconsistent.
