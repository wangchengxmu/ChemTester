# Organic nitro-subclass oxidizer and hazard comparison

**Retrieve with:** nitroalkane oxidizing strength, aliphatic versus aromatic nitro reactivity, organic nitro reactive-group hazards, nitro compounds reducing-agent incompatibility

**Use when:** A question compares oxidizing strength or incompatibility hazards among organic nitro subclasses, especially simple nitroalkanes and nitroaromatic compounds.

## Procedure

1. Identify the requested property as oxidizer or compatibility behavior; do not substitute acidity, nitronate formation, synthetic reactivity, or electrophilic-substitution behavior.
2. Classify each compound as a nitroalkane, nitroaromatic, nitrate or nitrite ester, or multiply nitrated material before comparing it.
3. For reactive-group comparisons of simple nitroalkanes with nitroaromatics, treat nitroalkanes as the milder oxidizing subclass.
4. Keep milder distinct from inert: nitroalkanes can still react violently with reducing agents at elevated temperature or pressure and can form hazardous salts with bases.
5. Translate the supported qualitative relation into the visible answer wording only after the chemical comparison is fixed.

## Guards

- Do not infer oxidizer strength solely from the nitrogen oxidation state or the presence of a nitro group.
- Do not use alpha-hydrogen acidity or nitronate chemistry to reverse an oxidizer-strength comparison.
- Do not equate milder with nonreactive.
- Do not extrapolate the simple-subclass ordering to nitrate esters or multiply nitrated energetic materials without compound-specific evidence.
