# Key Elements of Green Chemistry

## Source
[Key Elements of Green Chemistry (Lucia), Chapter 1](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Environmental_Chemistry/Key_Elements_of_Green_Chemistry_(Lucia)/01%3A_Key_Elements_of_Green_Chemistry)
Author: Lucian Lucia, North Carolina State University

## Key Concepts

### The 12 Principles of Green Chemistry (Anastas & Warner)
1. **Prevention**: It is better to prevent waste than to treat or clean up waste after it is formed
2. **Atom Economy**: Synthetic methods should maximize incorporation of all materials into final product
3. **Less Hazardous Synthesis**: Use and generate substances with minimal toxicity
4. **Designing Safer Chemicals**: Preserve efficacy while reducing toxicity
5. **Safer Solvents & Auxiliaries**: Avoid auxiliary substances when possible; use innocuous ones
6. **Design for Energy Efficiency**: Run reactions at ambient temperature and pressure
7. **Use of Renewable Feedstocks**: Prefer agricultural/biological sources over depleting fossil stocks
8. **Reduce Derivatives**: Avoid unnecessary blocking/protecting groups
9. **Catalysis**: Catalytic reagents superior to stoichiometric
10. **Design for Degradation**: Products should break down to innocuous degradation products
11. **Real-time Analysis for Pollution Prevention**: Monitor and control in real-time
12. **Inherently Safer Chemistry**: Minimize accident potential (explosions, fires, releases)

### Green Chemistry Metrics
- **E-factor** (Sheldon): mass waste / mass product (ideal = 0)
- **Atom Economy (AE)**: (MW product / Σ MW reactants) × 100%
- **Process Mass Intensity (PMI)**: total mass input / mass product
- **Carbon footprint**: CO₂ equivalent emissions per unit product

### Historical Context
- 1990 Pollution Prevention Act (US)
- 1998: 12 Principles published by Anastas & Warner
- EPA Presidential Green Chemistry Challenge Awards (since 1996)

## Formulas
- E-factor = (mass waste) / (mass product)
- Atom Economy = (MW of desired product / Σ MW of all reactants) × 100%
- PMI = (total mass in process) / (mass of product) = 1 + E-factor
