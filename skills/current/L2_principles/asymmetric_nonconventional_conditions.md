## 7.2 Reactions in Fluorous Solvents

Fluorous solvents (perfluorinated alkanes, ethers) are immiscible with conventional organic solvents, enabling **biphasic catalysis**.

### Key Properties
- **Phase behavior**: Immiscible with organics at RT; may become miscible at elevated temperature
- **Temperature-dependent miscibility**: Heat â?homogeneous reaction; cool â?phase separation for catalyst recovery
- **Catalyst immobilization**: Fluorous-tagged catalysts partition into fluorous phase

- General applicability to Rh, Pd, Cu-catalyzed asymmetric reactions with fluorous ligands

### Limitations
- High cost of fluorous solvents and reagents
- Environmental persistence of perfluorinated compounds (PFAS concerns)
- Limited commercial availability of fluorous-tagged ligands

## 7.5 Microwave-Assisted Reactions

Microwave irradiation provides **rapid, uniform heating** compared to conventional thermal methods, reducing reaction times from hours to minutes.

### Key Principles
- **Dielectric heating**: Microwaves couple directly with polar molecules, not the vessel
- **Rate enhancement**: Reduces reaction time, minimizes side reactions, improves yields
- **Selectivity impact**: Can change regioselectivity (Mo vs Pd allylic alkylation gives different isomers)

### Applications
- **Allylic alkylation**: Chiral Mo complex under microwave â?high ee in minutes
  - Pd system gives different regioisomer under same conditions (method-dependent selectivity)
- **Arylation of aldehydes**: Arylboronic acid + aldehyde + EtâZn + aziridine ligand â?98% ee
  - Time reduced from 1 h (conventional) to 15 min (microwave)
- Widely adopted in pharmaceutical industry for rapid reaction optimization

### Limitations
- Scale-up challenges (limited microwave penetration depth)
- Specialized equipment required
- Safety concerns with sealed vessels at elevated pressure
