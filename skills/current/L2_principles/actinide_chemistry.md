# Actinide Chemistry - Redox and Coordination

## Concept Overview

The actinides (elements 89-103) are all radioactive and exhibit rich redox chemistry with multiple accessible oxidation states. The 5f orbitals extend further from the nucleus than 4f, allowing more covalent bonding character compared to lanthanides.

**Source:** LibreTexts Inorganic Chemistry - Actinides

---

## Key Principles

### Definition
- Elements 89 (Ac) through 103 (Lr)
- F-block of period 7
- All elements are radioactive
- Early actinides (Th-Pu) have accessible multiple oxidation states
- Later actinides (Am onwards) predominantly +3

### General Properties
| Property | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Radioactivity | All unstable; α, β, γ emitters |
| Appearance | Silvery metals (when pure) |
| Reactivity | Very reactive, pyrophoric as powders |
| Toxicity | Radioactive + heavy metal toxicity |
| Coordination | High coordination numbers (6-12) |

### 5f Orbital Characteristics
- **More extended** than 4f (less penetration)
- **Greater overlap** with ligand orbitals
- **More covalent bonding** possible
- **Competes with 6d** in early actinides

## Redox Chemistry

### Uranium Redox Chemistry

#### Stability in Aqueous Solution
| Oxidation State | Stability |
|-----------------|-----------|
| U³⁺ | Reduces to U⁴⁺ by H₂O |
| U⁴⁺ | Stable in absence of air |
| U⁵⁺ | Disproportionates: 2U⁵⁺ → U⁴⁺ + U⁶⁺ |
| U⁶⁺ | Stable as UO₂²⁺ (uranyl ion) |

#### Uranyl Ion (UO₂²⁺)
- **Linear O=U=O** structure
- **Very stable** in aqueous solution
- **Yellow color** characteristic
- **Coordination** in equatorial plane (typically 4-6 ligands)

### Plutonium Redox Chemistry

#### Multiple Oxidation States
Pu can exist simultaneously in +3, +4, +5, +6 states in solution:
- **Pu³⁺:** Blue-violet
- **Pu⁴⁺:** Tan/brown
- **Pu⁵⁺:** Pink
- **Pu⁶⁺:** Pink-orange (as PuO₂²⁺)

#### Disproportionation Reactions
```
3Pu⁴⁺ + 2H₂O ⇌ 2Pu³⁺ + PuO₂²⁺ + 4H⁺
```

### Redox Potentials (E° values)

| Couple | E° (V) vs NHE |
|--------|---------------|
| U³⁺/U⁴⁺ | -0.61 |
| U⁴⁺/UO₂⁺ | +0.38 |
| UO₂⁺/UO₂²⁺ | +0.16 |
| Pu³⁺/Pu⁴⁺ | +1.01 |
| Pu⁴⁺/PuO₂⁺ | +1.04 |
| PuO₂⁺/PuO₂²⁺ | +0.94 |

## Coordination Chemistry

### Coordination Numbers and Geometries

| Oxidation State | Typical CN | Geometry |
|-----------------|------------|----------|
| +3 | 6-9 | Octahedral, tricapped trigonal prism |
| +4 | 8-10 | Dodecahedral, square antiprismatic |
| +5 | 6-8 | Various |
| +6 | 6-8 (uranyl) | Equatorial coordination |

### Uranyl Complexes
The linear O=U=O unit dominates U(VI) chemistry:

```
Structure of uranyl complexes:
     O
     ||
     U
     ||
     O
     
Ligands bind in equatorial plane
```

**Common uranyl complexes:**
- [UO₂(H₂O)₅]²⁺ - aqua ion
- [UO₂(NO₃)₃]⁻ - nitrate complex
- [UO₂(acac)₂] - β-diketonate
- [UO₂(CO₃)₃]⁴⁻ - carbonate (important in groundwater)

### Ligand Preferences

| Oxidation State | Preferred Ligands |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| +3 | Hard O-donors (similar to lanthanides) |
| +4 | O-donors, some N-donors |
| +5, +6 | O-donors, can accept π-backbonding |

### Important Actinide Complexes

**Thorium(IV):**
- ThO₂ (thoria) - high melting oxide
- ThCl₄ - halide
- Th(NO₃)₄·5H₂O - nitrate

**Uranium:**
- UF₆ - volatile, used for isotope separation
- UO₂(NO₃)₂ - uranyl nitrate
- UO₃, U₃O₈ - oxides

**Plutonium:**
- PuO₂ - dioxide (nuclear fuel)
- PuCl₃, PuCl₄ - halides

## Halide Chemistry

### Halide Types

**Actinium:**
- Only forms trihalides (AcX₃)

**Thorium:**
- ThX₄ (all tetrahalides)
- ThX₂, ThX₃ (lower oxidation states rare)

**Uranium:**
- U(III): UF₃, UCl₃, UBr₃, UI₃
- U(IV): UF₄, UCl₄, UBr₄, UI₄
- U(V): Rare
- U(VI): UF₆, UCl₆

### Uranium Hexafluoride (UF₆)
- **Colorless solid** at room temperature
- **Sublimes at 56.5°C** (atmospheric pressure)
- **Octahedral** molecular geometry
- **Used for isotope separation** (gas diffusion/centrifuge)
- **Made by:** U + 3F₂ → UF₆ or UF₄ + F₂ → UF₆

### Other Important Halides

**Neptunium:**
- NpF₆ - volatile, toxic
- NpCl₃, NpCl₄ - chlorides

**Plutonium:**
- PuF₄, PuF₆ (PF₆ very unstable)
- PuCl₃, PuCl₄

**Americium:**
- AmF₃, AmF₄
- AmCl₃, AmBr₃, AmI₃
- AmI₂ (dihalide becomes stable)

## Nuclear Properties

### Radioactive Decay
All actinides are radioactive with various decay modes:

| Decay Type | Description | Example |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| α decay | Emits ⁴He nucleus | ²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + α |
| β⁻ decay | n → p + e⁻ | ²³⁹U → ²³⁹Np + β⁻ |
| β⁺ decay | p → n + e⁺ | Rare in actinides |
| Spontaneous fission | Nucleus splits | ²⁴⁰Pu |

### Important Isotopes

| Isotope | Half-life | Use |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| ²³⁸U | 4.47 × 10⁹ y | Depleted uranium, fertile |
| ²³⁵U | 7.04 × 10⁸ y | Fissile, nuclear fuel |
| ²³⁹Pu | 2.41 × 10⁴ y | Fissile, weapons, fuel |
| ²³⁸Pu | 87.7 y | RTG power source |
| ²⁴¹Am | 433 y | Smoke detectors |
| ²³⁷Np | 2.14 × 10⁶ y | Neutron detector |

### Nuclear Fission

**²³⁵U fission:**
```
²³⁵U + ¹n → ²³⁶U → fission products + neutrons + energy
```

- Releases ~200 MeV per fission
- Produces 2-3 neutrons per fission
- Enables chain reaction

## Solubility and Precipitation

### Low Solubility Compounds
- **Fluorides:** AnF₃, AnF₄
- **Hydroxides:** An(OH)₃, An(OH)₄
- **Oxalates:** An₂(C₂O₄)₃
- **Phosphates:** AnPO₄

### Separation Applications
1. **Precipitate from acidic solution:** Phosphates
2. **Precipitate from mildly acidic:** Oxalates
3. **Precipitate from strong acid:** Fluorides
4. **Precipitate from basic:** Hydroxides

### Hydrogen Peroxide Precipitation
H₂O₂ precipitates:
- Pu⁴⁺, Th⁴⁺, Ce⁴⁺, U⁴⁺, Np⁴⁺, Pa⁴⁺

## Applications

### Nuclear Energy
- **Uranium:** Nuclear reactor fuel (²³⁵U enrichment)
- **Plutonium:** MOX fuel (mixed oxide)
- **Thorium:** Potential fertile material for breeding ²³³U

### Nuclear Weapons
- **²³⁵U:** Gun-type weapons
- **²³⁹Pu:** Implosion weapons

### Power Sources
- **²³⁸Pu RTGs:** Space missions, pacemakers
- Long-lived power without sunlight

### Industrial/Medical
- **²⁴¹Am:** Smoke detector ionization source
- **⁹⁹Mo/⁹⁹mTc generators:** Medical imaging (not actinide, but from reactor)

### Historical
- **ThO₂:** Gas mantles (Welsbach mantle)

## Handling and Safety

### Hazards
1. **Radioactivity:** α, β, γ radiation
2. **Chemical toxicity:** Heavy metal poisoning
3. **Criticality:** Chain reactions if mass/concentration exceeded
4. **Pyrophoricity:** Fine powders ignite in air

### Safety Measures
- **Glove boxes** for handling
- **Shielding** (α: paper, β: plastic, γ: lead)
- **Containment** of all materials
- **Criticality control** (geometry, moderation)
