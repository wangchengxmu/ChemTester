# Radical Chemistry — Graduate-Level Reference

## 1. Fundamentals

### 1.1 Radical Structure and Electronics
- **Free radical**: species with an unpaired electron (doublet, S = ½).
- **Carbon-centered radicals** are planar (sp²) or nearly planar; pyramidalization increases with substituents.
- **SOMO** (singly occupied molecular orbital): analogous to frontier orbitals in closed-shell chemistry; reactivity governed by SOMO energetics.
- **Classification**: nucleophilic radicals (electron-rich, lower SOMO, e.g., alkyl), electrophilic radicals (electron-poor, higher SOMO, e.g., acyl, α-carbonyl), ambiphilic radicals (e.g., α-heteroatom).

### 1.2 Stability Trends
**Order (carbon-centered):** tertiary (3°) > secondary (2°) > primary (1°) > methyl ·CH₃

- **Hyperconjugation**: more β-C–H bonds → greater stabilization (~1–3 kcal/mol per β-H).
- **Resonance stabilization**: allyl ≈ benzyl > 3° (delocalization provides ~10–12 kcal/mol stabilization).
  - Allyl radical: spin density distributed over two termini (SOMO = Ψ₂).
  - Benzyl radical: delocalized into aromatic π-system; ortho/para spin density.
- **α-Heteroatom stabilization**: radicals adjacent to N, O, S, or halogens are stabilized by n→p conjugation (capto effects). Order: α-N > α-O ≈ α-S > α-halogen.
- **Alkenyl radicals**: less stable than alkyl; vinyl radical (sp²) ≈ methyl radical.
- **Acyl radicals**: moderately electrophilic; stabilized by resonance with carbonyl.

### 1.3 Captodative Effect
- **Synergistic stabilization** when a radical center bears both an electron-withdrawing (acceptor) and electron-donating (donor) substituent simultaneously.
- Example: ·CH(CO₂Et)(NMe₂) — the donor raises, the acceptor lowers the SOMO, reducing spin density on carbon.
- Provides extra 3–8 kcal/mol stabilization beyond additive effects.
- Leads to persistent radical character and influences regioselectivity in addition reactions.

### 1.4 Persistent Radicals
- **Persistent radicals**: radicals with sufficient kinetic or thermodynamic stability to be isolated or observed (lifetimes from seconds to years).
  - **TEMPO** (2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine-1-oxyl): nitroxyl radical, sterically protected.
  - **Galvinoxyl**: sterically shielded phenoxyl radical, deep green, used as a radical inhibitor/trap.
  - **Triphenylmethyl (trityl) radical**: first observed organic radical (Gomberg, 1900); stability from steric delocalization.
  - **Verdazyl, nitronyl nitroxide, BDPA** radicals: stable organic radicals used in materials science.
- **Persistent Radical Effect (PRE)**: cross-coupling favored between a persistent radical (P·) and a transient radical (T·) because P· accumulates while T·–T· dimerizes; P· + T· cross-couples selectively. Key principle in radical polymerization and cross-coupling design (Fischer concept).

## 2. Radical Generation

### 2.1 Homolytic Bond Cleavage — Initiators

| Initiator | BDE (kcal/mol) | Conditions | Key Use |
|-----------|---------------|------------|---------|
| **Benzoyl peroxide (BPO)** | PhCOO–OCOPh ~30 | 70–100 °C | Classical initiator |
| **AIBN** (azobisisobutyronitrile) | (CH₃)₂C(CN)–N=N–C(CN)(CH₃)₂ ~31 | 60–80 °C | General radical initiator |
| **Di-tert-butyl peroxide (DTBP)** | (CH₃)₃CO–OC(CH₃)₃ ~37 | 120–150 °C | High-temp initiator |
| **VA-044** (V-50) | Water-soluble azo | 40–60 °C | Aqueous radical reactions |
| **BEt₃/O₂** | — | rt, air | Tin-free initiation |

**Mechanism (AIBN example):**
1. Thermal cleavage: AIBN → 2 ·C(CH₃)₂CN + N₂
2. Cyanoisopropyl radical initiates chain (typically H-atom abstraction from tin hydride or substrate).

**Photolysis initiators:**
- **Benzophenone** (n→π* excitation at 350 nm): forms ketyl radical after HAT.
- **Diacetyl** (455 nm), **Irgacure 2959** (UV).
- **Acyl persulfides**, **N-hydroxyphthalimide esters** (NHPI esters) under photoredox.

### 2.2 Tin Hydride Mediation
- **Tributyltin hydride (Bu₃SnH)**: the classic radical chain transfer agent.
- **Mechanism:**
  1. Initiator (AIBN) → In· (radical)
  2. In· + R–X → R· + In–X (halogen atom abstraction, R–X BDE is key)
  3. **R· + Bu₃SnH → R–H + Bu₃Sn·** (HAT, rate-determining for many reactions, k ≈ 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹ for primary radicals)
  4. Bu₃Sn· + R'–X → Bu₃SnX + R'· (chain propagation)

- **Reactivity order for R–X abstraction by Bu₃Sn·**: I > Br > Cl > F; allylic/benzylic > alkyl > acyl.
- **Problem**: tin residues are difficult to remove (environmental and purification concerns).
- **Alternatives**: (TMS)₃SiH (tris(trimethylsilyl)silane), Bu₃GeH, polymethylhydrosiloxane (PMHS).

### 2.3 Silane-Mediated Radical Reactions
- **(TMS)₃SiH**: k(HAT) ≈ 10⁵–10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹; slower than Bu₃SnH but much less toxic.
- **PhSiH₃** (phenylsilane): cheaper, used in photoredox HAT catalysis.
- Silane radical cations (generated by SET from excited photocatalyst to silane) are powerful H-atom donors.

### 2.4 Hydrogen Atom Transfer (HAT) Catalysis
- **Direct HAT**: catalyst abstracts H· directly from substrate C–H bond.
- **Key HAT catalysts**:
  - **Decatungstate anion** [W₁₀O₃₂]⁴⁻ (TBADT): abstracts H from unactivated C(sp³)–H bonds (BDE up to ~100 kcal/mol) under 365 nm irradiation; generates carbon radicals and reduced decatungstate.
  - **Amino acids** (e.g., glycine, under photoredox oxidation to aminoacyl radical), **thiols**, **quinuclidine**.
  - **Mn³⁺ porphyrins**, **Fe³⁺ complexes**.
- **Selectivity**: governed by C–H BDE and steric accessibility; weakest C–H bond abstracted preferentially.

### 2.5 Single Electron Transfer (SET)
- **Oxidative SET**: substrate → substrate⁺· (radical cation). Reductive SET: substrate → substrate⁻· (radical anion).
- **Common SET agents**: SmI₂, Na/Hg, Cp₂TiCl, photoredox catalysts, electrochemical reduction.
- **Key substrates for reductive SET**: alkyl halides (→ R· + X⁻), esters/ketones (→ ketyl radicals), aryl halides (via Ni or photoredox).

### 2.6 Barton–McCombie Deoxygenation
- **Purpose**: convert alcohols → alkanes (deoxygenation via radical pathway).
- **Procedure**:
  1. R–OH → R–OC(=S)NHPh (xanthate, via base + CS₂ + PhNCS)
  2. R–OC(=S)NHPh + Bu₃SnH + AIBN → R–H + byproducts

- **Mechanism**:
  1. AIBN → In·
  2. In· + Bu₃SnH → Bu₃Sn· + In–H
  3. Bu₃Sn· + xanthate → R–C(=O)S· (radical) + Bu₃SnS(O)CNHPh
  4. R–C(=O)S· → R· + COS (fragmentation, rapid, BDE of C–S bond is weak ~30 kcal/mol)
  5. R· + Bu₃SnH → R–H + Bu₃Sn·

- Advantages over ionic reduction: chemoselective, tolerates esters, ethers, ketones; stereochemistry at adjacent centers may scramble (radical planarization).

## 3. Radical Propagation

### 3.1 Hydrogen Atom Transfer (Chain Propagation)
- **Rate constants** for HAT to carbon radicals:
  - Bu₃Sn· + R–H: k ≈ 10⁵–10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹ (depends on radical and C–H BDE)
  - Thiol (RS·) + R–H: k ≈ 10⁶–10⁷ M⁻¹s⁻¹ (thiol-ene mechanism, reversible)
  - Fast and reversible for thiols → anti-Markovnikov selectivity in thiol-ene reactions.

### 3.2 Addition to π-Systems — Giese Reaction
- **Giese reaction**: radical addition to electron-deficient alkenes (Michael acceptors) followed by HAT or other trapping.
  - R· + CH₂=CH–EWG → R–CH₂–CH·–EWG (new C–C bond formed)
  - Then: R–CH₂–CH·–EWG + H–donor → R–CH₂–CH₂–EWG

- **EWGs**: CO₂R, COR, CN, SO₂R, CONR₂, etc.
- **Regioselectivity**: radical adds to β-position (terminal) of electron-deficient alkene, forming the more stable α-EWG radical.
- **Rate constants** (kₐdd, M⁻¹s⁻¹, for methyl radical):
  - CH₂=CHCO₂Et: ~10⁸
  - CH₂=CHCN: ~10⁸
  - CH₂=CHSO₂Ph: ~10⁹
  - CH₂=CH₂ (unactivated): ~10⁵
  - C₆H₅CH=CH₂ (styrene): ~10⁷

- **Reversibility**: addition to unactivated alkenes is often reversible; addition to activated alkenes is typically irreversible.
- **Stereoselectivity**: typically non-stereoselective (planar radical); may show Felkin-Anh-like selectivity with chiral auxiliaries.

### 3.3 Fragmentation (β-Scission)
- **β-Scission**: R¹–R²· → R¹· + R²= (or R¹= + R²·)
- Favored when the resulting radical is more stable than the starting one.
- **Ring-opening of cyclopropylmethyl radical**: cyclopropylmethyl · → homoallyl radical (ring opens, k ≈ 10⁸ s⁻¹). This is the basis of the cyclopropylmethyl radical clock.
- **Fragmentation in Barton decarboxylation**: Barton PTOC (pyridine-2-thione N-oxycarbonyl) esters fragment to give radicals upon photolysis or thermolysis.

### 3.4 Rearrangements

#### Neophyl Rearrangement
- **Migration**: aryl group migrates from α to β position of a radical center.
- **General**: Ar–CH₂–CH₂· → ·CH₂–CH₂–Ar (1,2-aryl migration via bridged intermediate)
- **Rate**: k ≈ 10⁵–10⁶ s⁻¹ at 25 °C (aryl = phenyl)
- Ring size effect: 5-membered transition state → fast; 6-membered → slower.

#### Brook Rearrangement (Radical Version)
- **Silyl group migration**: R₃Si–C·(R')–OR → R₃Si–O–C·(R')–R
- Radically-triggered 1,2-silyl migration from carbon to oxygen.
- Related to the classical anionic Brook rearrangement.

#### 1,5-Hydrogen Atom Transfer (1,5-HAT)
- **Intramolecular HAT**: translocation of a radical center via 1,5-H shift.
  - H–C–C–C–C–C· → ·C–C–C–C–C–H (six-membered transition state)
- **Preferred over 1,4- or 1,6-HAT** due to favorable geometry (pseudo-chair TS).
- Rate: k ≈ 10³–10⁵ s⁻¹ depending on C–H BDE.
- Key step in Hofmann–Löffler–Freytag reaction and related C–H functionalization.

### 3.5 Cyclization — Baldwin Rules
- **Baldwin rules for radical ring closure**:
  - **5-exo-trig**: favored (k ≈ 10⁵ s⁻¹ at 25 °C for 5-hexenyl)
  - **6-exo-trig**: also favored (k ≈ 10⁵ s⁻¹ for 6-heptenyl, comparable to 5-exo for many systems)
  - **3-exo-trig, 4-exo-trig**: disfavored (high strain)
  - **endo vs exo**: exo generally favored; 5-exo > 6-endo, but 6-endo becomes competitive with stabilizing substituents at the radical terminus.

- **5-Hexenyl radical cyclization** (the benchmark):
  - k(5-exo) ≈ 2.2 × 10⁵ s⁻¹ at 25 °C
  - k(6-endo) ≈ 5 × 10³ s⁻¹ at 25 °C
  - Ratio: ~44:1 exo:endo

- **Steric and electronic effects**: aryl or EWG at the radical terminus can reverse exo/endo preference.

## 4. Radical Clocks

### 4.1 Principle
- A **radical clock** is a molecule that generates a radical which rearranges or fragments with a known rate constant (k). By measuring the ratio of rearranged vs trapped products, the rate of the competing reaction (e.g., HAT from Bu₃SnH) can be determined.

### 4.2 Calibration Equation
$$\frac{[\text{rearranged}]}{[\text{trapped}]} = \frac{k_r}{k_t[\text{H-donor}]}$$

Where $k_r$ = rearrangement rate, $k_t$ = HAT rate constant.

### 4.3 Common Radical Clocks

| Clock | k (s⁻¹) at 25 °C | Reference |
|-------|------------------|-----------|
| Cyclopropylmethyl → homoallyl | ~2 × 10⁸ | Ring opening |
| 5-Hexenyl cyclization (5-exo) | ~2.2 × 10⁵ | Ring closure |
| 2,2-Dimethyl-5-hexenyl | ~3 × 10⁶ | Ring closure |
| Neophyl (Ph migration) | ~5 × 10⁵ | 1,2-shift |
| (E)-1-Phenyl-5-hexenyl (endo) | ~4 × 10⁴ | Ring closure |
| Bicyclo[2.2.1]hept-2-yl → rearranged | ~10⁶ | Ring opening |
| Acyloxy radical decarboxylation | ~10⁹ | CO₂ loss |
| Phenyl radical addition to benzene | ~10⁹–10¹⁰ | ipso addition |

### 4.4 Applications
- Determining absolute rate constants for HAT reactions.
- Proving radical intermediates (if clocked rearrangement product is observed).
- Establishing chain lengths in radical reactions.
- Comparing relative reactivity of different H-donor systems.

## 5. Inter- and Intramolecular Radical Reactions

### 5.1 Carbonyl Addition (Giese-Type Addition to Aldehydes/Ketones)
- Alkyl radicals can add to the carbonyl group, but this is generally **slower** than addition to activated alkenes.
- **Enantioselective radical addition to carbonyls**: developed using chiral Lewis acids (e.g., chiral samarium complexes, chiral oxazaborolidines).
- **Single electron transfer pathway** to carbonyl → ketyl radical → protonation → alcohol.

### 5.2 Allylation Reactions
- **Radical allylation**: R· + CH₂=CH–CH₂X → R–CH₂–CH=CH₂ + X· (via allyl radical intermediate, resonance-delocalized)
- **Allyl stannane method**: R–X + Bu₃SnH + CH₂=CHCH₂SnBu₃ → R–CH₂–CH=CH₂ (allylstannane as radical trap)
- **Regioselectivity**: radical adds to terminal carbon of allyl system; product depends on which terminus the halogen atom leaves.

### 5.3 Atom Transfer Radical Addition (ATRA)
- **General**: R–X + alkene → R–alkene–X (anti-Markovnikov addition of R–X across C=C)
- **Mechanism**:
  1. Initiator → In·
  2. In· + R–X → R· + In–X
  3. R· + CH₂=CHR' → R–CH₂–C·HR' (addition to alkene)
  4. R–CH₂–C·HR' + R'–X → R–CH₂–CHXR' + R'· (atom transfer, chain propagation)

- **Catalytic ATRA**: uses Cu, Fe, or Ru complexes as mediators.
  - **CuCl/bpy**: R–CH₂–C·HR' + Cu(II)Cl₂ → R–CH₂–CHClR' + Cu(I)Cl
  - **Kharasch addition**: the classic example — CHCl₃ + alkene → CHCl₂–CH₂–CHCl–R (Cu-catalyzed).

### 5.4 Atom Transfer Radical Polymerization (ATRP)
- **Controlled/living radical polymerization**: uses ATRA equilibrium to maintain low steady-state radical concentration.
- **Mechanism**:
  - Activator Cu(I)X/L + R–Pₙ–X (dormant) ⇌ Deactivator Cu(II)X₂/L + R–Pₙ· (active)
  - Active radical propagates briefly before being deactivated → controlled molecular weight, low dispersity (Đ ≈ 1.1–1.3).

- **ATRP variants**:
  - **Normal ATRP**: Cu(I)/L initiates, Cu(II) accumulates.
  - **Reverse ATRP**: conventional radical initiator + Cu(II) → Cu(I) by reduction.
  - **ARGET ATRP** (activators regenerated by electron transfer): uses reducing agent (Sn(EH)₂, ascorbate, Cu(0)) to regenerate Cu(I) from Cu(II).
  - **eATRP** (electrochemical): Cu(II) electrochemically reduced to Cu(I) at cathode.

- **Monomers**: acrylates, methacrylates, styrenes, acrylamides.
- **Key advantage**: tolerance to functional groups, predictable Mₙ, block copolymer synthesis.

### 5.5 Nitroxide-Mediated Polymerization (NMP)
- TEMPO or alkoxyamine-mediated living radical polymerization.
- Dormant alkoxyamine → radical + nitroxyl (thermally reversible).
- **TIPNO, SG1**: more versatile nitroxides (lower kd, broader monomer scope).

## 6. Modern Methods

### 6.1 Photoredox Catalysis

#### Principles
- **Excited-state photocatalyst** undergoes SET with substrate → radical species.
- **Stokes shift**: catalyst absorbs visible light, emits at longer wavelength.
- **Redox potentials** (vs SCE) determine accessible substrates.

#### Common Photocatalysts

| Catalyst | E½(*PC/PC⁻) (V) | E½(PC⁺/PC*) (V) | Excited-state lifetime | λmax (nm) |
|----------|------------------|------------------|----------------------|-----------|
| **[Ru(bpy)₃]²⁺** (Ru) | +0.77 | −0.81 | ~1.1 µs | 452 |
| **[Ir(ppy)₃]** (Ir) | +0.31 | −1.73 | ~1.9 µs | 376 |
| **Ir[dF(CF₃)ppy]₂(dtbbpy)⁺** | +1.21 | −1.37 | ~2.3 µs | 460 |
| **4CzIPN** (organic) | +1.35 | −1.21 | ~1 µs | 430 |
| **Eosin Y** (organic) | +0.83 | −1.11 | ~0.02 µs | 525 |
| **9-Mesityl-10-methylacridinium** | +2.06 | — | — | 430 |

#### Mechanism Types
1. **Oxidative quenching**:
   - PC* + substrate (reductant) → PC⁻ + substrate⁺·
   - PC⁻ + oxidant (e.g., Ni(II) or O₂) → PC + product
2. **Reductive quenching**:
   - PC* + substrate (oxidant) → PC⁺ + substrate⁻·
   - PC⁺ + reductant (e.g., amine, Hantzsch ester) → PC + byproduct

### 6.2 Electrochemical Radical Generation
- **Anodic oxidation**: substrate → substrate⁺· (radical cation) at anode.
  - Example: oxidation of carboxylic acids → alkyl radicals via decarboxylation.
- **Cathodic reduction**: substrate → substrate⁻· (radical anion) at cathode.
  - Example: R–Br + e⁻ → R· + Br⁻ (reductive cleavage).
- **Undivided cells** (paired electrolysis) allow both oxidation and reduction simultaneously.
- **Advantages**: no chemical oxidant/reductant; controlled by applied potential; scalable.
- **Key examples**:
  - **Shono oxidation**: electrochemical oxidation of amides → α-amino radicals.
  - **Baran electrochemical C–H functionalization**: decarboxylative coupling of NHPI esters.
  - **Kolbe electrolysis**: 2 RCO₂⁻ → R–R + 2 CO₂ (radical dimerization at anode).

### 6.3 Dual Catalysis — Photoredox + Transition Metal

#### Photoredox/Nickel Dual Catalysis (MacMillan, Doyle, Molander)
- **General scheme**:
  1. Photoredox: R–X + e⁻ (from PC⁻) → R· + X⁻ (reduction of alkyl halide)
  2. Ni(0) + R–X → Ni(II)–R + X⁻ (oxidative addition)
  3. R· + Ni(II)–Ar → Ni(III)–Ar–R (radical capture by Ni)
  4. Ni(III)–Ar–R → Ar–R + Ni(I) (reductive elimination)
  5. PC⁺ + Ni(I) → PC + Ni(II) (catalyst regeneration)

- **Scope**: C(sp²)–C(sp³), C(sp²)–C(sp²), C(sp³)–N, C(sp³)–S, C(sp³)–O couplings.
- **Substrates**: alkyl halides, organotrifluoroborates, carboxylic acids (via NHPI esters), aryl halides/pseudohalides.

#### Other Dual Catalysis
- **Photoredox + Cu**: Cu(I)-catalyzed radical addition to Michael acceptors, Chan–Lam-type couplings.
- **Photoredox + Enzyme**: chemoenzymatic radical cascades (e.g., using Old Yellow Enzyme + photocatalyst).
- **Photoredox + HAT catalyst**: dual catalysis for C–H functionalization.

### 6.4 Decarboxylative Couplings
- **NHPI ester activation** (redox-active esters, RAEs):
  - RCO₂NHPI + e⁻ (photoredox or electrochemical) → RCO₂⁻· → R· + CO₂
  - R· then participates in Ni-catalyzed cross-coupling, addition to alkenes, or Minisci-type reactions.
- **N-Hydroxyphthalimide (NHPI) esters**: prepared from carboxylic acids via DCC coupling.
- **Scope**: primary, secondary, tertiary acids; amino acids (giving α-amino radicals).
- **Key example**: decarboxylative Suzuki–Miyaura coupling (Baran, 2014).

### 6.5 C–H Functionalization via HAT
- **HAT catalyst + oxidant/radical trap**:
  - Substrate C–H → ·C (HAT) → ·C + trap → functionalized product
- **Selective remote C–H functionalization** via 1,5-HAT:
  - Hofmann–Löffler–Freytag: N-centered radical abstracts δ-H, then halogenation.
  - Suárez reaction: I₂/hν or PhI(OAc)₂/I₂ for remote C–H iodination.
  - Chen's decatungstate-mediated C–H alkylation.
- **Recent advances**:
  - **Ceric ammonium nitrate (CAN)** for HAT from aliphatic C–H.
  - **Magnetically biased HAT** for enantioselective C–H functionalization (List, Prier).
  - **Bio-inspired iron catalysts** for selective C–H oxidation (White, Groves).

## 7. Radical Reactions in Synthesis

### 7.1 Radical Cascades
- **Tandem cyclization sequences**: radical generates, adds to alkene, cyclizes, then traps.
  - Example: polyene cyclization via multiple 5-exo-trig steps.
- **Iminyl radical cascades**: oxime derivatives → iminyl radical → β-scission → alkene radical → cyclization.
  - **Nitrile synthesis**: from oxime esters via photoredox → iminyl → fragmentation → nitrile.
- **Key features**: rapid, bond-forming events (typical step: ~10⁵–10⁸ s⁻¹), high step-economy.

### 7.2 Macrocyclization
- **Radical cyclization for macrocycle formation**: 5-exo → 6-endo competitive, but for medium/large rings, endo/exo competition is less relevant.
- **Advantages over ionic cyclization**: high dilution not required (radical concentrations are inherently low); tolerance to many functional groups.
- **Example**: 10–20 membered lactone formation via 5-hexenoyl-derived radicals.

### 7.3 Late-Stage Functionalization (LSF)
- **C–H abstraction** on complex molecules (natural products, drugs):
  - Decatungstate (365 nm) → site-selective HAT → radical trapped by Michael acceptor or halogen.
  - Site selectivity governed by C–H BDE and accessibility.
- **Photoredox decarboxylation** of native carboxylic acids in complex molecules.
- **Minisci reaction**: radical addition to protonated heteroarenes → C–H functionalization of drug-like heterocycles (quinolines, isoquinolines, pyridines).
  - Mechanism: R· + [HetH]⁺ → R–Het·⁺ → oxidation + deprotonation → R–Het⁺ → R–Het.
- **Examples**: functionalization of strychnine, quinine, felodipine, finasteride.

## 8. Radical Traps and Inhibitors

### 8.1 TEMPO (2,2,6,6-Tetramethylpiperidine-1-oxyl)
- **Nitroxyl radical**: persistent, sterically hindered, purple solid.
- **Mechanism of inhibition**: TEMPO couples with carbon radicals (R· + TEMPO → R–O–N• → R–O–N–R or R–O–N(OH)–R after disproportionation), terminating the chain.
- **Rate constant**: k ≈ 10⁸–10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹ for coupling with alkyl radicals.
- **Uses**: radical scavenger in polymerization inhibition, spin label for EPR, mediator in TEMPO oxidation of alcohols (Cu/TEMPO system).

### 8.2 Galvinoxyl
- **Phenoxyl radical**: deep green color, strong radical inhibitor.
- **Mechanism**: similar to TEMPO — fast radical-radical coupling.
- **Often used** as a radical test: addition of galvinoxyl to a reaction quenches radical pathways, confirming radical mechanism.

### 8.3 BHT (Butylated Hydroxytoluene, 2,6-di-tert-butyl-4-methylphenol)
- **Phenolic antioxidant**: chain-breaking antioxidant via HAT to peroxyl radicals.
- **Mechanism**: BHT + ROO· → ROOH + BHT· (stable phenoxyl radical).
- **BHT·** is resonance-stabilized and sterically shielded → does not propagate.
- **Used as**: food preservative, polymer stabilizer, radical reaction inhibitor.

### 8.4 Other Traps
- **1,1-Diphenylethylene**: radical trap that gives a diagnostic colored adduct.
- **Proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) traps**: e.g., cobaloximes, phenols.
- **Molecular oxygen**: O₂ is a diradical (triplet) that reacts rapidly with carbon radicals → peroxyl radicals (ROO·), which can lead to autoxidation or terminate chains.

## 9. Worked Examples

### Example 1: Tin Hydride Reduction of Alkyl Bromide
**Substrate**: 1-bromohexane → hexane (Bu₃SnH, AIBN, 80 °C, benzene)

**Steps**:
1. AIBN → 2 ·C(CH₃)₂CN + N₂
2. ·C(CH₃)₂CN + Bu₃SnH → (CH₃)₂CHCN + Bu₃Sn·
3. Bu₃Sn· + Br(CH₂)₅CH₃ → Bu₃SnBr + ·(CH₂)₅CH₃
4. ·(CH₂)₅CH₃ + Bu₃SnH → CH₃(CH₂)₅H + Bu₃Sn·

**Chain length** ≈ 20–50; the ratio of [Bu₃SnH]/[AIBN] ≈ 10–20:1.

---

### Example 2: 5-Hexenyl Radical Cyclization
**Substrate**: 6-bromo-1-hexene → methylcyclopentane (Bu₃SnH, AIBN)

**Steps**:
1. Bu₃Sn· + Br(CH₂)₄CH=CH₂ → Bu₃SnBr + ·(CH₂)₄CH=CH₂
2. ·(CH₂)₄CH=CH₂ → cyclopentylmethyl radical (**5-exo-trig**, k ≈ 2.2 × 10⁵ s⁻¹)
3. cyclopentylmethyl· + Bu₃SnH → cyclopentylmethyl–H + Bu₃Sn·

**Product ratio**: cyclopentylmethyl (exo, ~97%) vs cyclohexyl (endo, ~3%).

---

### Example 3: Barton–McCombie Deoxygenation
**Substrate**: 1-octanol → octane

**Steps**:
1. 1-Octanol + NaH + CS₂ → octyl OCS₂⁻Na⁺
2. Octyl OCS₂⁻ + PhNCS → octyl xanthate (OC(=S)SPh)
3. Octyl xanthate + Bu₃SnH + AIBN → octane + Bu₃Sn–S(C=O)NPh

**Key step**: ·OC(=S)SPh radical → ·C₈H₁₇ + COS (fast β-scission, drives the reaction irreversibly).

---

### Example 4: Giese Reaction — Addition to Methyl Acrylate
**Substrate**: ethyl iodoacetate + methyl acrylate → dimethyl succinate (Et₃B/O₂ initiation)

**Steps**:
1. Et₃B + O₂ → Et₃B–OO· (initiation)
2. Et₃B–OO· + ICH₂CO₂Et → Et₃B–OOI + ·CH₂CO₂Et
3. ·CH₂CO₂Et + CH₂=CHCO₂Me → CH₃CH(CO₂Et)CH₂CO₂Me· (radical addition)
4. New radical + R–H (H-donor) → CH₃CH(CO₂Et)CH₂CO₂Me + R· (chain)

### Example 5: Neophyl Rearrangement — Radical Clock
**Substrate**: PhCH₂CH₂· (from PhCH₂CH₂Br + Bu₃Sn·)

**Competing pathways**:
- **Rearrangement**: PhCH₂CH₂· → ·CH₂CH₂Ph (k ≈ 5 × 10⁵ s⁻¹, 1,2-phenyl migration)
- **HAT**: PhCH₂CH₂· + Bu₃SnH → PhCH₂CH₃ + Bu₃Sn· (k_t ≈ 2 × 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹)

At [Bu₃SnH] = 0.1 M: [rearranged]/[trapped] = k_r/(k_t × [SnH]) = (5 × 10⁵)/(2 × 10⁶ × 0.1) = 2.5
→ ~71% rearranged product, confirming radical intermediate.

### Example 6: Atom Transfer Radical Addition (ATRA)
**Substrate**: CCl₄ + 1-octene → 1:1 adduct (CuCl/bpy catalysis, 60 °C)

**Steps**:
1. Cu(I)Cl + CCl₄ → Cu(II)Cl₂ + ·CCl₃ (initiation)
2. ·CCl₃ + CH₂=CH(CH₂)₅CH₃ → Cl₃C–CH₂–CH·(CH₂)₅CH₃
3. Cl₃C–CH₂–CH·(CH₂)₅CH₃ + Cu(II)Cl₂ → Cl₃C–CH₂–CHCl(CH₂)₅CH₃ + Cu(I)Cl

**Product**: 1:1 anti-Markovnikov adduct (Cl₃C–CH₂–CHCl–C₆H₁₃).

### Example 7: Photoredox Decarboxylative Coupling
**Substrate**: phenylacetic acid NHPI ester + 4-iodotoluene → 4-methylbibenzyl

**Catalyst**: Ir[dF(CF₃)ppy]₂(dtbbpy)PF₆ + NiCl₂·glyme + dtbbpy + DIPEA, blue LEDs

**Steps**:
1. Ir(III)* + DIPEA → Ir(II) + DIPEA⁺· (reductive quenching)
2. Ir(II) + PhCH₂CO₂NHPI → Ir(III) + PhCH₂CO₂⁻ + NHPI· (SET)
3. PhCH₂CO₂⁻· → PhCH₂· + CO₂ (decarboxylation)
4. PhCH₂· + Ni(II)(bpy)(Ar–I)(I) → Ni(III)(bpy)(Ar)(CH₂Ph)(I) (radical capture)
5. Ni(III)(bpy)(Ar)(CH₂Ph)(I) → Ar–CH₂Ph + Ni(I)(bpy)(I) (reductive elimination)
6. Ni(I) + Ir(III) → Ni(II) + Ir(II) (catalyst regeneration)

### Example 8: Hofmann–Löffler–Freytag Reaction
**Substrate**: N-chloropiperidine → δ-chloropiperidine

**Steps**:
1. N–Cl bond homolysis (heat/hν): >N–Cl → >N· + Cl·
2. 1,5-HAT: >N· abstracts δ-H → >NH + ·CH₂– (at C-4)
3. ·CH₂– + Cl₂ (or Cl· source) → CH₂Cl (at C-4)
4. Subsequent base treatment → Δ¹-piperideine (pyrrolidine after reduction)

### Example 9: Minisci Reaction
**Substrate**: quinoline + CH₃CN + (NH₄)₂S₂O₈, AgNO₃, 60 °C

**Steps**:
1. S₂O₈²⁻ → 2 SO₄⁻· (thermal or Ag-catalyzed homolysis)
2. SO₄⁻· + CH₃CN → CH₂CN· + HSO₄⁻ (HAT, or CN· loss from oxime derivative)
3. CH₂CN· + quinoline-H⁺ (protonated at N) → addition at C-2 or C-4 → radical cation adduct
4. Oxidation (by Ag(II) or SO₄⁻·) + deprotonation → 2-(cyanomethyl)quinoline

**Regioselectivity**: C-2 > C-4 for quinoline (most electron-deficient positions).

### Example 10: Decatungstate-Mediated C–H Alkylation
**Substrate**: cyclohexane + methyl acrylate → 3-cyclohexylpropanoate

**Catalyst**: TBADT [W₁₀O₃₂]⁴⁻ (1 mol%), 365 nm UV

**Steps**:

2. TBADT* + cyclohexane → [TBADT–H]³⁻ + cyclohexyl· (HAT)
3. Cyclohexyl· + CH₂=CHCO₂Me → cyclohexyl–CH₂–CH·CO₂Me (Giese addition)
4. Cyclohexyl–CH₂–CH·CO₂Me + [TBADT–H]³⁻ → cyclohexyl–CH₂–CH₂CO₂Me + TBADT⁴⁻ (HAT, chain propagation)

### Example 11: Iminyl Radical Cascade
**Substrate**: ketoxime ester (O-acetyl ketoxime) → nitrile via β-scission

**Catalyst**: Ir(ppy)₃, blue LEDs

**Steps**:
1. Ir(III)* + R₂C=N–OAc → Ir(IV) + R₂C=N–OAc⁻· (SET reduction)

3. Iminyl radical β-scission: R–C(=N·)–CH₂–R' → R–C≡N + ·CH₂R' (nitrile formation)

### Example 13: Photoredox α-Amino C–H Alkylation
**Substrate**: N-phenylpyrrolidine + methyl acrylate

**Catalyst**: Ru(bpy)₃Cl₂ (1 mol%), visible light

**Steps**:
1. Ru(bpy)₃²⁺ + hν → Ru(bpy)₃²⁺* (MLCT excited state)
2. Ru(bpy)₃²⁺* + amine (N-phenylpyrrolidine) → Ru(bpy)₃⁺ + amine⁺· (SET oxidation)
3. Amine⁺· deprotonation → α-amino radical (at C-2 of pyrrolidine)
4. α-Amino· + CH₂=CHCO₂Me → addition product radical
5. Product radical + Ru(bpy)₃⁺ → product anion + Ru(bpy)₃²⁺ (SET) → protonation → β-amino ester

### Example 14: ATRP of Methyl Methacrylate
**Substrate**: MMA → PMMA, controlled molecular weight

**Catalyst**: CuBr/PMDETA, initiator: ethyl 2-bromoisobutyrate (EBiB)

**Steps**:
1. EBiB + Cu(I)Br/L → Cu(II)Br₂/L + ·C(CH₃)(CO₂Et)CH₂Br (activation)
2. ·C(CH₃)(CO₂Et)CH₂Br + n MMA → growing radical chain (propagation)
3. Growing radical + Cu(II)Br₂/L → dormant chain–Br + Cu(I)Br/L (deactivation)
4. Activation/deactivation equilibrium → low [radical], controlled growth

**Result**: PMMA with Đ < 1.2, Mₙ = ([MMA]₀/[EBiB]₀) × conversion × M_MMA.

### Example 15: Late-Stage C–H Functionalization of Strychnine
**Substrate**: strychnine + ethyl acrylate → C-12 alkylated strychnine

**Catalyst**: TBADT (2 mol%), 365 nm, ethyl acrylate (10 equiv)

**Steps**:
1. TBADT + hν → TBADT*
2. TBADT* abstracts H from C-12 of strychnine (weakest accessible C–H, BDE ≈ 96 kcal/mol)
3. C-12 radical of strychnine + CH₂=CHCO₂Et → C-12-alkylated radical
4. HAT from [TBADT–H]³⁻ → product + TBADT (regeneration)

**Selectivity**: C-12 > C-20 > C-22, governed by C–H BDE and steric access in the rigid cage structure.
