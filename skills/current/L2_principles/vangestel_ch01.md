# Environmental Toxicology

## Source
[Environmental Toxicology (van Gestel et al.), Chapter 1](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Environmental_Chemistry/Environmental_Toxicology_(van_Gestel_et_al.)/01%3A_Environmental_Toxicology)
Authors: Sylvia Moes, Kees van Gestel, & Gerco van Beek

## Key Concepts

### Environmental Toxicology Definition
- Study of harmful effects of chemical, physical, and biological agents on living organisms and ecosystems
- Distinct from ecotoxicology: focuses on environmental (non-human) organisms
- Distinguishes from human toxicology (occupational, clinical)

### Historical Context
- Silent Spring (Rachel Carson, 1962): catalyzed environmental toxicology as a discipline
- Classic cases: DDT bioaccumulation, Minamata mercury poisoning, Love Canal
- Growing awareness of chemical contamination in environment

### Key Subdisciplines
- **Ecotoxicology**: effects on populations, communities, ecosystems
- **Environmental chemistry**: behavior and fate of chemicals in the environment
- **Risk assessment**: quantitative evaluation of hazards and exposures
- **Environmental forensics**: identifying sources and responsibility for contamination

### Scope and Scales
- Molecular/cellular level → individual → population → community → ecosystem
- Spatial scales: local (contaminated site) → regional (watershed) → global (climate change)
- Temporal scales: acute exposure → chronic exposure → multigenerational effects

### Classes of Environmental Contaminants
- Metals (Pb, Hg, Cd, As)
- Persistent organic pollutants (POPs): PCBs, dioxins, DDT
- Pesticides and herbicides
- Pharmaceuticals and personal care products (PPCPs)
- Nanomaterials and microplastics (emerging contaminants)

## Formulas
- Risk = Hazard × Exposure
- Dose-response: LD₅₀, LC₅₀, EC₅₀, NOEC, LOEC
