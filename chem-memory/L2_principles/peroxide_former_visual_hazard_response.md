# Peroxide-Forming Ether Visible-Crystal Hazard Response

Peroxide-forming chemicals can accumulate explosive peroxides during storage.
Ethers are a common peroxide-forming class. Peroxidized material may be
sensitive to shock, friction, or heat.

## Initial Visual Screen

Before a peroxide-forming chemical is opened, moved for use, or tested, inspect
the container without disturbing it. Treat any of the following as a failed
initial screen:

- crystallization in the liquid, around the cap, or on the container;
- discoloration;
- liquid stratification or phase separation;
- abnormal deposits, crusts, or other visible degradation;
- unknown age or origin when safe storage history cannot be established.

Visible crystallization, discoloration, or stratification can indicate that a
peroxide former has become shock-sensitive. A container that fails this screen
is unsafe and must not proceed to ordinary peroxide testing.

## Immediate Action

For a container that fails the visual screen:

1. Do not touch, move, open, sample, test, clean, cool, or otherwise disturb it.
2. Never force a rusted or stuck cap and never remove deposits from the closure.
3. Keep other personnel from handling the container according to the site's
   emergency or hazardous-material procedure.
4. Contact the institution's environmental health and safety or
   hazardous-materials emergency service promptly for specialist assessment,
   stabilization, removal, and disposal.

Disposal in this context means expert-managed disposition. It is not permission
for routine laboratory personnel to pick up or transport the container.

## Testing Versus Disposal

Routine peroxide testing applies only after the container passes the initial
screen. A normally appearing, in-date container may be tested according to the
institution's schedule and procedure. An expired container without visible
degradation still requires the site's peroxide-former protocol.

Visible degradation is different: do not test the material to decide whether it
can be reused. In multiple-choice questions, do not reinterpret an option about
testing or assessing continued use as equivalent to contacting specialists for
safe disposal.

## Scope Guard

Apply this hard-stop rule when the material is a peroxide former or the
laboratory-storage context makes peroxide formation plausible. Crystallization
in an unrelated chemical does not by itself establish a peroxide hazard.
