## Definition

The **lanthanide series** comprises the 14 elements from Cerium (Ce, Z=58) to Lutetium (Lu, Z=71), following Lanthanum (La, Z=57). These elements are characterized by progressive filling of the 4f orbitals and are collectively known as the **4f elements** or **rare earth metals** (together with scandium and yttrium).

---

## Core Concepts

### 1. Electron Configuration

After Lanthanum, the energy of the 4f subshell falls below that of the 5d subshell, so electrons begin filling the 4f orbital.

**General configuration:** [Xe] 4f^n 5d^0-1 6s^2

| Element | Symbol | Idealized Config | Observed Config |
|---------|--------|------------------|-----------------|
| La | La | 5d¹6s² | 5d¹6s² |
| Ce | Ce | 4f¹5d¹6s² | 4f¹5d¹6s² |
| Pr | Pr | 4f²5d¹6s² | 4f³6s² |
| Nd | Nd | 4f³5d¹6s² | 4f⁴6s² |
| Pm | Pm | 4f⁴5d¹6s² | 4f⁵6s² |
| Sm | Sm | 4f⁵5d¹6s² | 4f⁶6s² |
| Eu | Eu | 4f⁶5d¹6s² | 4f⁷6s² |
| Gd | Gd | 4f⁷5d¹6s² | 4f⁷5d¹6s² |
| Tb | Tb | 4f⁸5d¹6s² | 4f⁹6s² |
| Dy | Dy | 4f⁹5d¹6s² | 4f¹⁰6s² |
| Ho | Ho | 4f¹⁰5d¹6s² | 4f¹¹6s² |
| Er | Er | 4f¹¹5d¹6s² | 4f¹²6s² |
| Tm | Tm | 4f¹²5d¹6s² | 4f¹³6s² |
| Yb | Yb | 4f¹³5d¹6s² | 4f¹⁴6s² |
| Lu | Lu | 4f¹⁴5d¹6s² | 4f¹⁴5d¹6s² |

**Key observations:**
- Gd (4f⁷) and Lu (4f¹⁴5d¹) have half-filled and fully-filled f-subshell stability
- Deviations from idealized configurations due to f-d energy proximity

---

### 2. Oxidation States

**Dominant oxidation state:** +3 (most stable and common for all lanthanides)

**Why +3 dominates:**
- Removal of two 6s and one 5d/4f electron gives noble gas configuration
- Further ionization requires very high energy (removing from 4f)

**Non-+3 Oxidation States**

| Element | +2 State | +4 State | Reason |
|---------|----------|----------|--------|
| Ce | - | Stable | f⁰ configuration (Ce⁴⁺) |
| Pr | - | Less stable | f¹ (Pr⁴⁺) |
| Nd | - | Rare | - |
| Sm | Exists | - | f⁶ (Sm²⁺) |
| Eu | Stable | - | f⁷ (Eu²⁺), half-filled |
| Gd | - | - | - |
| Tb | - | Exists | f⁷ (Tb⁴⁺), half-filled |
| Dy | - | Rare | - |
| Yb | Stable | - | f¹⁴ (Yb²⁺), filled |

**Stability Rules:**
1. **Half-filled or filled f subshell** favors that oxidation state
2. **Eu²⁺ (f⁷)** and **Yb²⁺ (f¹⁴)** are stable due to closed shells
3. **Ce⁴⁺ (f⁰)** and **Tb⁴⁺ (f⁷)** are stable due to empty/half-filled f

### 3. Lanthanide Contraction

#### Definition
The steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La to Lu due to poor shielding of 4f electrons.

#### Cause
1. **Poor 4f shielding:** f orbitals have poor penetrating power
2. **Increasing nuclear charge:** As protons are added, Zeff increases
3. **No new shell:** Electrons added to 4f, not a new outer shell
4. **5s and 5p penetration:** Outer orbitals penetrate 4f, feel increased Zeff

#### Shielding Effectiveness
```
Shielding ability: s > p > d > f
(Greatest)                    (Least)
```

#### Atomic and Ionic Radii

| Element | Atomic Radius (pm) | Ionic Radius Ln³⁺ (pm) |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------|
| La | 187.7 | 106.1 |
| Ce | 182 | 103.4 |
| Pr | 182.8 | 101.3 |
| Nd | 182.1 | 99.5 |
| Pm | 181 | 97.9 |
| Sm | 180.2 | 96.4 |
| Eu | 204.2 | 95.0 |
| Gd | 180.2 | 93.8 |
| Tb | 178.2 | 92.3 |
| Dy | 177.3 | 90.8 |
| Ho | 176.6 | 89.4 |
| Er | 175.7 | 88.1 |
| Tm | 174.6 | 89.4 |
| Yb | 194.0 | 85.8 |
| Lu | 173.4 | 84.8 |

**Total decrease:** ~15 pm from La³⁺ to Lu³⁺

#### Consequences of Lanthanide Contraction

##### 1. Similar Radii in d-Block Row 5 vs Row 6
- Pd (Row 5) and Pt (Row 6) have nearly identical radii
- Expected: Pt >> Pd
- Observed: Pt ≈ Pd due to 4f electron addition

##### 2. Chemical Similarity
- Makes separation of lanthanides difficult
- Ion exchange needed for purification
- Subtle size differences allow selective complexation

##### 3. Increased Density and Hardness
- Density, melting point, and hardness increase across series
- Lu is denser and harder than La

##### 4. Ionization Energy Trend
- I₁ increases from La to Lu
- Higher Zeff → harder to remove electrons

### 4. Physical Properties

| Property | Trend |
|----------|-------|
| **Atomic radius** | Decreases from La to Lu |
| **Ionic radius (Ln³⁺)** | Decreases from 106.1 pm (La³⁺) to 84.8 pm (Lu³⁺) |
| **Melting point** | Generally increases across series |
| **Density** | Increases across series |
| **Ionization energy** | Increases across series |

**General Characteristics:**
- Silvery-white metals, tarnish quickly in air
- React slowly with cold water, faster when heated
- Dissolve readily in acids
- Oxidize rapidly in moist air
- Electropositive nature (similar to Group 2 metals)

---

### 5. Chemical Properties

#### Metal Reactivity
- **Silvery appearance** when freshly cut
- **Tarnish quickly** in moist air
- **Reactions:**
  - Slowly with cold water, faster when heated
  - Rapidly with acids
  - With oxygen: slow at RT, ignites at 150-200°C
  - With halogens: upon heating
  - With S, H, C, N: upon heating

#### Basicity Series
Basicity decreases with increasing atomic number:
```
La³⁺ > Ce³⁺ > Pr³⁺ > Nd³⁺ > Pm³⁺ > Sm³⁺ > Eu³⁺ > Gd³⁺ > 
Tb³⁺ > Dy³⁺ > Ho³⁺ > Er³⁺ > Tm³⁺ > Yb³⁺ > Lu³⁺
```

**Effect on chemistry:**
- Separation by fractional crystallization
- Solubility differences in salts
- Complex formation preferences

### 6. Coordination Chemistry

#### Coordination Numbers and Geometries

| Oxidation State | Typical CN | Geometry |
|-----------------|------------|----------|
| +3 | 8-9 (up to 12) | Tricapped trigonal prism, dodecahedral |

**Key features:**
- **High coordination numbers** (typically 8-9, up to 12)
- **Ionic bonding dominates** (little covalent character)
- **Little ligand field stabilization** (LFSE negligible)
- **Rapid ligand exchange** (labile complexes)
- **Preference for hard donor atoms** (O, F)

#### Preferred Ligands
- **Hard Lewis bases** (O, F donors)
- **Common ligands:** H₂O, OH⁻, carboxylates, β-diketonates
- **Chelating ligands:** EDTA forms very stable complexes

#### Typical Complexes
```
[Lanthanide(III) aqua ion]: [Ln(H₂O)₈₋₉]³⁺
EDTA complex: [Ln(EDTA)(H₂O)₃]⁻
β-diketonate: Ln(acac)₃(H₂O)₃
```

### 7. Magnetic Properties

#### Paramagnetic Ions
Most Ln³⁺ ions are paramagnetic due to unpaired 4f electrons.

| Ion | 4f electrons | Unpaired | Spin-only μ (BM) |
|-----|--------------|----------|------------------|
| La³⁺ | 0 | 0 | 0 (diamagnetic) |
| Ce³⁺ | 1 | 1 | 2.54 |
| Pr³⁺ | 2 | 2 | 3.58 |
| Nd³⁺ | 3 | 3 | 3.62 |
| Pm³⁺ | 4 | 4 | 2.68 |
| Sm³⁺ | 5 | 5 | 1.55 |
| Eu³⁺ | 6 | 6 | 3.40 |
| Gd³⁺ | 7 | 7 | 7.94 |
| Tb³⁺ | 8 | 6 | 9.7 |
| Dy³⁺ | 9 | 5 | 10.6 |
| Ho³⁺ | 10 | 4 | 10.4 |
| Er³⁺ | 11 | 3 | 9.6 |
| Tm³⁺ | 12 | 2 | 7.6 |
| Yb³⁺ | 13 | 1 | 4.5 |
| Lu³⁺ | 14 | 0 | 0 (diamagnetic) |

#### Spin-Orbit Coupling
- **Very strong** in lanthanides
- **J (total angular momentum)** determines magnetic moment
- **Orbital contribution not quenched** (unlike d-block)
- **Temperature-dependent** magnetic behavior

### 8. Color and Spectroscopy

#### Colors of Ln³⁺ Ions

| Ion | Unpaired e⁻ | Color (aq) |
|-----|-------------|------------|
| La³⁺ | 0 | Colorless |
| Ce³⁺ | 1 | Colorless |
| Pr³⁺ | 2 | Green |
| Nd³⁺ | 3 | Reddish/violet |
| Pm³⁺ | 4 | Pink/yellow |
| Sm³⁺ | 5 | Yellow |
| Eu³⁺ | 6 | Pale pink |
| Gd³⁺ | 7 | Colorless |
| Tb³⁺ | 6 | Pale pink |
| Dy³⁺ | 5 | Yellow |
| Ho³⁺ | 4 | Pink/yellow |
| Er³⁺ | 3 | Reddish/pink |
| Tm³⁺ | 2 | Green |
| Yb³⁺ | 1 | Colorless |
| Lu³⁺ | 0 | Colorless |

#### f-f Transitions
- **Laporte-forbidden** (Δl = 0, same parity)
- **Very sharp lines** (f orbitals shielded from ligands)
- **Low intensity** (ε ~ 1-10 M⁻¹cm⁻¹)
- **Weak ligand field effects**

#### Luminescence

**Key property:** Many lanthanide ions exhibit sharp, line-like emission due to f-f transitions.

**Important luminescent ions:**
- **Eu³⁺** - Red emission (~615 nm)
- **Tb³⁺** - Green emission (~545 nm)
- **Dy³⁺** - Yellow emission
- **Sm³⁺** - Orange emission
- **Ce³⁺** - UV emission (5d→4f transition)

**Applications:**
- Phosphors in displays and lighting
- Fluorescent lamps
- LED lighting
- Anti-counterfeiting inks
- Biomedical imaging

### 9. Occurrence and Separation

#### Mineral Sources
- **Monazite:** Light lanthanides (La-Nd), phosphate mineral
- **Xenotime:** Heavy lanthanides (Tb-Lu)
- **Bastnäsite:** Light lanthanides, fluoride carbonate

#### Oddo-Harkins Rule
Elements with **even atomic number** are more abundant than adjacent odd-numbered elements (greater nuclear stability).

#### Separation Methods

##### 1. Solvent Extraction
- Uses organophosphorus compounds (TBP, D2EHPA)
- Separation factor ~2-3 between adjacent lanthanides
- Multiple stages required

##### 2. Ion Exchange Chromatography
- α-hydroxyisobutyric acid eluent
- Heavy lanthanides elute first

##### 3. Selective Oxidation/Reduction
- Ce: Oxidize to Ce⁴⁺, precipitate
- Eu: Reduce to Eu²⁺, separate

---

### 10. Applications

#### Magnets
- **Nd-Fe-B magnets:** Strongest permanent magnets
- **Sm-Co magnets:** High temperature stability

#### Phosphors and Displays
- **Eu³⁺:** Red phosphor (CRT, LED)
- **Tb³⁺:** Green phosphor
- **Ce³⁺:** Blue phosphor

#### Catalysis
- **Fluid catalytic cracking:** La, Ce exchanged zeolites
- **Automotive catalysts:** CeO₂ oxygen storage

#### Other Uses
- **Glass polishing:** CeO₂
- **Lasers:** Nd:YAG, Er-doped fiber amplifiers
- **Petroleum refining:** La, Ce catalysts
- **Nuclear:** Gd for neutron absorption

## Common Lanthanide Compounds

### Oxides
- Formula: Ln₂O₃ (most common)
- Exceptions: CeO₂, Pr₆O₁₁, Tb₄O₇
- Basic oxides, absorb CO₂ from air

### Halides
- LnX₃ (most common, X = F, Cl, Br, I)
- LnF₃ is insoluble (used in separation)
- CeF₄, TbF₄ exist (M⁴⁺ oxidation state)

### Hydroxides
- Ln(OH)₃ - gelatinous precipitates
- Basicity decreases across series
- Precipitate at lower pH for heavier lanthanides

### Coordination Complexes
- High coordination numbers (8-10 typical)
- Common ligands: EDTA, DTPA, β-diketonates, crown ethers
- Organometallics: Cp₃Ln, (C₅H₅)₃Ln

## Comparison: Lanthanides vs Actinides

| Property | Lanthanides | Actinides |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| f orbital | 4f | 5f |
| Oxidation states | +3 dominant | +3 to +7 |
| Radioactivity | None (stable) | All radioactive |
| Bonding | Primarily ionic | More covalent |
| f orbital exposure | Buried | More exposed |
| Coordination | High CN, ionic | High CN, more covalent |
