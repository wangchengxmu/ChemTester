# NMR Signal Counting: Branched Aldehydes And Equivalent Groups

Use this note when a question asks how many distinct `1H NMR` or `13C NMR`
signals appear for a branched aldehyde, tert-butyl-containing aldehyde, or
formula pattern such as `(CH3)3CCH2CHO`.

## Procedure

1. Draw the carbon skeleton and mark symmetry-equivalent atoms before counting
   signals.
2. Equivalent methyl groups on a tert-butyl-like `(CH3)3C-` fragment give one
   methyl proton environment and one methyl carbon environment.
3. A quaternary carbon has no attached protons, but it still gives a distinct
   signal in normal broadband-decoupled `13C NMR`.
4. The `-CH2-CHO` portion normally contributes a methylene proton environment
   and an aldehydic proton environment for `1H NMR`.
5. For `13C NMR`, count the equivalent methyl carbons, the quaternary carbon,
   the methylene carbon, and the aldehyde carbonyl carbon as separate carbon
   environments unless an additional symmetry element makes them equivalent.

## Guards

- Count environments, not individual atoms or individual hydrogens.
- Do not use splitting patterns to count the number of environments unless the
  prompt asks about multiplicity.
- Aldehyde carbonyl carbons are carbon environments even though they have no
  directly attached proton.
