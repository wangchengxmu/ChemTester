# Rate ranking from secondary deuterium isotope effects

**Retrieve with:** inverse secondary deuterium isotope effect, electrophilic alkene bromination KIE, halonium formation isotope effect, remote deuteration reaction rate, steric vibrational isotope effect

**Use when:** Comparing rates of electrophilic addition to isotopologues when the labeled C-H or C-D bonds are not cleaved, especially in rigid or crowded alkenes.

## Procedure

1. Map every H/D label relative to the reacting center and ask whether its bond is broken in the rate-determining step; if it remains intact, analyze a secondary rather than primary isotope effect.
2. State the ratio convention: for k_H/k_D, a value below one is inverse and means the deuterated isotopologue is faster.
3. For electrophilic alkene halogenation, evaluate the transition toward halonium or charge development and changes in coupled C-H/C-D bending and stretching modes; rigid or crowded frameworks can give inverse effects even at homoallylic sites.
4. Rank isotopologues by the direction and number of comparable contributing labels; combine supported independent site effects multiplicatively, or use only a qualitative cumulative ranking when magnitudes are unavailable.
5. If the sign for a label class is not established, retrieve a close reaction-class study and preserve uncertainty instead of defaulting to a universal deuterium slowdown.

## Guards

- Do not use the large normal primary isotope-effect rule when no labeled bond is cleaved.
- Do not treat remote labels as automatically irrelevant or count all deuteriums without mapping site coupling.
- Do not explain inverse effects as literal atomic size alone; use zero-point vibrational and steric-mode changes.
- Do not multiply site effects unless equivalence or independence is justified.
