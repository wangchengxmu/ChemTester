# Heteronuclear and long-range coupling in proton NMR multiplicity

**Retrieve with:** proton fluorine NMR coupling, heteronuclear proton splitting, long-range J HF coupling, 19F decoupling proton NMR

**Use when:** A proton-NMR multiplicity problem contains fluorine, phosphorus, or another spin-active heteronucleus, especially when a signal appears to lack neighboring protons or the nuclei are connected by a plausible long-range or constrained close-contact pathway.

## Procedure

1. Assign the likely signal from chemical shift and integration before determining its multiplicity.
2. Inventory all spin-active coupling partners, not only hydrogens; trace plausible through-bond paths and constrained close contacts that can transmit scalar coupling.
3. For an ordinary proton spectrum, retain heteronuclear coupling unless decoupling, exchange, or insufficient resolution is stated or strongly implied.
4. Split sequentially by each inequivalent coupling set: one coupled spin-1/2 nucleus produces a doublet, while additional distinct couplings produce patterns such as doublets of doublets.
5. Check the proposed multiplicity against coupling-path plausibility, experimental decoupling, and whether the expected J value should be resolved.

## Guards

- Do not apply a proton-only n+1 rule when a coupled spin-active heteronucleus is present.
- Do not infer scalar splitting from proximity or an NOE alone; an observable J-coupling mechanism is required.
- Do not suppress heteronuclear splitting unless decoupling or non-resolution is supported.
- Do not count equivalent protons within the observed group as nuclei that split one another.
