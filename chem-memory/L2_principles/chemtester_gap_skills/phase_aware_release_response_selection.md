# Phase-aware release hazard and response selection

**Retrieve with:** confined-space gas hazard, gas accumulation oxygen deficiency, toxicity versus asphyxiation, phase-aware release hazards

**Use when:** A qualitative chemical-safety question asks for the most direct hazard or applicable response associated with gas or vapor accumulation, especially in a confined or poorly ventilated space.

## Procedure

1. Determine whether the stem asks about an intrinsic substance property or a hazard created by the stated phase, accumulation, ventilation, and surroundings.
2. Map the visible choices to mechanisms such as oxygen depletion, dose-dependent poisoning, ignition, corrosive contact, or sensory nuisance.
3. Rank choices by direct support from the stated scenario: confined gas accumulation supports an oxygen-deficient atmosphere, while toxicity, ignition, or corrosion require corresponding substance-specific or situational evidence.
4. Select the single most directly entailed category rather than every plausible secondary hazard, then preserve the requested answer format.

## Guards

- Do not let a merely plausible intrinsic toxicity override the mechanism explicitly established by the scenario.
- Do not classify every confined-space gas as a simple oxygen displacer when the stem supplies specific toxicant, oxygen-reactive, or ignition evidence.
- Do not promote odor, corrosion, or another secondary property without the exposure pathway or contact condition that makes it directly relevant.
