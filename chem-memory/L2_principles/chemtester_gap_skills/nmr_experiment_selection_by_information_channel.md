# NMR experiment selection by nucleus and diagnostic contrast

**Retrieve with:** NMR experiment selection, fluorine-19 NMR, nucleus-specific NMR, NOESY versus heteronuclear NMR, stereoisomer discrimination NMR

**Use when:** An NMR planning or multiple-choice task asks which experiment best distinguishes structures differing in stereochemistry, substitution, or a heteronuclear probe environment.

## Procedure

1. Identify the structural difference and list the spin-active nuclei whose local environments or couplings it changes.
2. Rank direct observation of an affected, sensitive, well-dispersed nucleus highly; fluorine-19 is especially informative when fluorine environments differ because it has full natural abundance, high sensitivity, and broad chemical-shift dispersion.
3. Match the alternative experiments to their actual information channels: proton spectra report proton environments, HMBC reports long-range bond connectivity, and NOESY reports specific through-space proton contacts.
4. Choose NOESY for stereochemical discrimination only when a concrete diagnostic proton-proximity contrast is expected; otherwise prefer the simplest direct experiment with the clearest predicted spectral difference.
5. State the predicted distinguishing feature and why the nearest alternative is less direct or more ambiguous.

## Guards

- Do not choose NOESY merely because the prompt mentions stereochemistry.
- Do not choose a heteronuclear experiment solely because that element is present; verify that the compared structures change its environment or coupling pattern.
- Enantiomers ordinarily have identical spectra in an achiral medium; require a chiral medium, derivatization, or another chiral discrimination mechanism.
- Account for symmetry and conformational averaging before claiming distinct shifts, couplings, or NOE contacts.
