# Condition-coupled spin, electron-configuration, and exact-geometry audit

**Retrieve with:** four-coordinate exact geometry, spin geometry consistency, tetrahedral Jahn-Teller distortion, pressure-induced spin crossover, metal carbonyl electron counting

**Use when:** A coordination or organometallic problem couples electron-count rules, magnetic state, valence-orbital occupation, or an exact geometry claim across pressure, temperature, or another ligand-field perturbation.

## Procedure

1. Build a condition-by-condition table separating total valence electrons or EAN, metal-centered configuration, unpaired-electron count, coordination number, and exact geometry or symmetry.
2. Count electrons independently of geometry; unchanged composition can preserve total electron count while pressure or ligand-field changes redistribute electrons between ns and d orbitals.
3. For each proposed geometry, fill its symmetry-specific orbital levels and compare the predicted spin. For high-spin tetrahedral d6, e3t2^3 gives four unpaired electrons but an orbitally degenerate, distortion-prone state, so regular Td requires direct support.
4. For four-coordinate species, compare tetrahedral, distorted or seesaw, and square-planar candidates; when spin or orbital occupation changes, test both geometry-preserving and geometry-changing models against structural, spectroscopic, or theoretical evidence.
5. Evaluate every statement independently, then map the exact truth set to the requested answer representation.

## Guards

- Do not use EAN or the 18-electron rule as a geometry proof.
- Do not equate coordination number four or high spin with a regular tetrahedron.
- Do not transfer octahedral spin tables unchanged to tetrahedral, square-planar, or low-symmetry structures.
- Do not infer an exact point group from diamagnetism alone.
