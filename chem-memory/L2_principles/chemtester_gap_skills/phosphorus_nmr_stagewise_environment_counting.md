# Stagewise phosphorus stereochemistry and 31P NMR environment counting

**Retrieve with:** phosphorus stereogenicity 31P NMR, phosphorochloridate hydrolysis, phosphate nonbridging oxygen equivalence, diastereomeric phosphorus signals

**Use when:** A reaction-monitoring or structure question asks how proton-decoupled phosphorus NMR signal counts change as substituents at tetrahedral phosphorus are replaced or hydrolyzed.

## Procedure

1. Draw the phosphorus substituent set at each named stage and determine constitution, charge or protonation, symmetry, and exchange on the NMR timescale before counting signals.
2. When tetrahedral phosphorus has four nonequivalent ligand paths, enumerate its configurations; with fixed stereocenters or a chiral environment, opposite phosphorus configurations are diastereomeric and may give separate resonances.
3. After hydrolysis to an ordinary phosphate diester monoanion, treat the two nonbridging oxygens as equivalent through delocalized charge and bonding; phosphorus configurational chirality is lost unless isotopic or elemental substitution distinguishes those oxygens.
4. Count chemically distinct phosphorus environments at each endpoint, separate signal count from chemical-shift movement, and only then map the direction of change to the requested answer.
5. Treat simultaneous reactant and product resonances at partial conversion as a transient mixture rather than the endpoint pattern.

## Guards

- Do not infer resonance count from stereoisomer count alone: enantiomers coincide in an achiral medium, whereas diastereomers can separate.
- Proton decoupling removes scalar splitting by protons; it does not merge chemically nonequivalent phosphorus environments.
- Do not freeze one Lewis form of a phosphate anion and treat its nonbridging oxygens as different.
- Flag unusual pH, isotopic labeling, slow exchange, aggregation, or incomplete conversion when these could change the observed pattern.
