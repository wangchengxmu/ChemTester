# Equilibrium-Limited Catalytic Conversion Triage

**Retrieve with:** equilibrium-limited reactor conversion, catalyst versus equilibrium, reaction enthalpy temperature leverage, reversible outlet composition plateau

**Use when:** A reversible catalytic reaction gives the same outlet composition after catalyst loading or catalyst-composition changes, and proposed remedies use temperature or catalyst changes to raise per-pass conversion.

## Procedure

1. Treat an invariant outlet composition despite adequate activity and catalyst changes as evidence for an equilibrium plateau, while checking whether stated residence-time or transport limitations contradict that diagnosis.
2. Separate kinetics from thermodynamics: a catalyst can accelerate equilibration and alter selectivity but cannot change the equilibrium constant at fixed thermodynamic conditions.
3. Balance the reaction and assess actual equilibrium levers using reaction-enthalpy sign and magnitude, gas-mole change, feed activities, pressure, and product removal.
4. Apply a van't Hoff magnitude check; for nearly thermoneutral bond-exchange reactions, modest temperature changes cannot be assumed to produce a meaningful equilibrium shift.
5. Compare the attainable shift with the requested magnitude and constraints; if no proposed lever can materially move equilibrium, accept the per-pass plateau or consider separation and recycle rather than more catalyst.

## Guards

- Do not infer that a reaction is exothermic or endothermic from its name or catalyst behavior.
- Do not claim that more catalyst changes equilibrium conversion.
- Do not confuse a favorable shift direction with a substantial change; magnitude-check the equilibrium constant.
- Do not recommend temperature changes without considering their effects on rate, selectivity, and side reactions.
