# Peroxide-former recognition, concentration hazards, and response

**Retrieve with:** aldoxime peroxide hazard, peroxide former distillation, autoxidation peroxide impurities, reaction-class specificity

**Use when:** A safety or mechanism question links air exposure, evaporation, purification, or distillation to explosion, especially for oximes or other peroxide-forming organics, and candidate answers mix a broad reaction class with a more specific causal pathway.

## Procedure

1. Identify the exact reactive class and distinguish peroxide formation from unrelated oxidizer incompatibility; for oximes, distinguish aldehyde-derived from ketone-derived members when relevant.
2. Build the causal chain: oxygen exposure can form peroxide species, evaporation or distillation can concentrate less-volatile energetic impurities and add heat, and peroxide decomposition can initiate an explosion.
3. Rank candidate mechanisms by causal specificity. When generic oxidation and a specific peroxide-generating pathway are both offered, use the specific pathway if it directly explains the stated concentration or distillation hazard.
4. Check alternatives against their necessary conditions: hydrolysis requires an appropriate aqueous pathway, polymerization requires polymerizable functionality or a documented alert, and generic oxidation alone does not identify the energetic impurity.
5. Normalize recognizable spelling variants during reasoning, then preserve the requested answer representation.

## Guards

- Do not treat a specific peroxide-forming pathway and its umbrella oxidation class as independent competing mechanisms.
- Do not infer that every oxidizable organic compound is a peroxide former; require class-specific or substance-specific evidence.
- Keep peroxide formation separate from its later concentration, heating, and initiation during processing.
- For handling recommendations, defer to authoritative substance-specific safety guidance rather than mechanism alone.
