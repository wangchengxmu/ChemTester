## Context

Groups 13-16 span the diagonal line separating metals from nonmetals, showing the greatest diversity in chemical behavior. These groups include:
- **Group 13 (Boron family)**: B, Al, Ga, In, Tl
- **Group 14 (Carbon family)**: C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb
- **Group 15 (Nitrogen family)**: N, P, As, Sb, Bi
- **Group 16 (Oxygen family)**: O, S, Se, Te, Po

Key phenomena: **inert-pair effect**, **catenation**, **multiple bonding**, **metalloid behavior**.

## Group 13: The Boron Family

### Elements
B (nonmetal), Al (metal), Ga (metal), In (metal), Tl (metal)

### Key Properties

| Element | Atomic # | Valence Config | IE₁ (kJ/mol) | χ | Oxidation | Oxide Type |
|---------|----------|----------------|--------------|---|-----------|------------|
| B | 5 | 2s²2p¹ | 801 | 2.0 | +3 | Acidic |
| Al | 13 | 3s²3p¹ | 578 | 1.6 | +3 | Amphoteric |
| Ga | 31 | 4s²4p¹ | 579 | 1.8 | +3 | Amphoteric |
| In | 49 | 5s²5p¹ | 558 | 1.8 | +3 | Amphoteric |
| Tl | 81 | 6s²6p¹ | 589 | 1.8 | **+1** | Basic |

### Anomalies Explained

**Ga, In, Tl anomalies** arise from poor shielding by filled (n-1)d¹⁰ and (n-2)f¹⁴ subshells:
- Higher Z_eff than expected
- IE(Ga) > IE(Al)
- IE(Tl) > IE(In)

**Inert-pair effect in Tl**: Tl prefers +1 oxidation state because the 6s² electrons are reluctant to participate in bonding.

### Boron Chemistry

#### Elemental Boron
- B₁₂ icosahedra (multicenter bonding)
- Prepared: BCl₃ + 3/2 H₂ → B + 3HCl

#### Boron Compounds

| Compound | Formula | Key Properties |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| Boron nitride | BN | Graphite-like; cubic form like diamond |
| Boron oxide | B₂O₃ | Forms borosilicate glass (Pyrex) |
| Boron trihalides | BX₃ | Strong Lewis acids (electron-deficient) |
| Diborane | B₂H₆ | Three-center two-electron bonds |
| Boron carbide | B₄C | Armor plates, extremely hard |

#### Boron Hydrides (Boranes)
- B₂H₆: ΔH_comb = −2165 kJ/mol (high energy fuel potential)
- **Electron-rich species** (BH₄⁻): reductants
- **Electron-deficient species** (B₂H₆): oxidants

### Aluminum Chemistry

#### Extraction (Hall-Héroult Process)
```
Bauxite (Al₂O₃) → [Al(OH)₄]⁻ (NaOH) → Al(OH)₃ (CO₂) → Al₂O₃ → Al (electrolysis)
```

#### Aluminum Compounds

| Compound | Formula | Uses |
|----------|---------|------|
| Alumina | Al₂O₃ | Ceramics, gemstones (ruby, sapphire) |
| Aluminum chloride | Al₂Cl₆ | Lewis acid, Friedel-Crafts |
| Alums | M⁺M³⁺(SO₄)₂·12H₂O | Water purification |

### Heavier Group 13

| Element | Key Properties |
|---------|----------------|
| Ga | mp 29.7°C, liquid at slightly above RT; thermometers |
| In | Low-melting solders, transparent ITO electrodes |
| Tl | Toxic; +1 oxidation state preferred |

**Halide dimers**: Al₂Cl₆, Ga₂Cl₆, In₂Cl₆ form via bridging halogens.

## Group 14: The Carbon Family

### Elements
C (nonmetal), Si (metalloid), Ge (metalloid), Sn (metal), Pb (metal)

### Key Properties

| Element | Atomic # | Valence Config | IE₁ (kJ/mol) | χ | Oxidation | Oxide Type |
|---------|----------|----------------|--------------|---|-----------|------------|
| C | 6 | 2s²2p² | 1087 | 2.6 | +4 | Acidic |
| Si | 14 | 3s²3p² | 787 | 1.9 | +4 | Amphoteric |
| Ge | 32 | 4s²4p² | 762 | 2.0 | +4 | Amphoteric |
| Sn | 50 | 5s²5p² | 709 | 2.0 | +4, +2 | Amphoteric |
| Pb | 82 | 6s²6p² | 716 | 1.8 | **+2** | Basic |

### Key Trends

1. **Catenation decreases** down group
   - C-C bond: 356 kJ/mol (strong)
   - Si-Si bond: 222 kJ/mol (weaker)
   
2. **+2 oxidation state stability increases** (inert-pair effect)

3. **E=E and E≡E bonds weaken** down group (poor π overlap)

### Carbon Chemistry

#### Allotropes
| Allotrope | Structure | Properties |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Diamond | 3D tetrahedral | Hardest natural substance |
| Graphite | Layered sheets | Conductive, lubricant |
| Fullerenes | C₆₀ cages | Molecular carbon |
| Nanotubes | Rolled graphene | High strength, conductive |

#### Carbon Compounds

**Carbon oxides:**
- CO: Toxic, binds hemoglobin
- CO₂: Acidic oxide, greenhouse gas

**Carbides (three types):**

| Type | Examples | Reaction with H⁺ |
|------|----------|------------------|
| Ionic (methide) | Be₂C, Al₄C₃ | → CH₄ |
| Ionic (acetylide) | CaC₂, Na₂C₂ | → C₂H₂ |
| Interstitial | WC, Fe₃C | No reaction |
| Covalent | SiC, B₄C | No reaction |

**Tetrahalide stability:** CF₄ > CCl₄ > CBr₄ > CI₄ (decreases with halogen size)

### Silicon Chemistry

#### Unique Si-O Bond
- Si-O: ~452 kJ/mol (stronger than C-O at 358 kJ/mol!)
- **Reason**: Partial π character from O lone pairs → Si d orbitals
- **Result**: Silicon-oxygen compounds dominate Si chemistry

#### Silicate Structures

| Type | SiO₄⁴⁻ Sharing | Formula Unit | Example |
|------|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| Island (nesosilicate) | 0 O | SiO₄⁴⁻ | Olivine |
| Pairs (sorosilicate) | 1 O | Si₂O₇⁶⁻ | Epidote |
| Chains (inosilicate) | 2 O | (SiO₃²⁻)ₙ | Pyroxenes |
| Double chains | 2-3 O | (Si₄O₁₁⁶⁻)ₙ | Amphiboles |
| Sheets (phyllosilicate) | 3 O | (Si₂O₅²⁻)ₙ | Micas, clays |
| Framework (tectosilicate) | 4 O | (SiO₂)ₙ | Quartz |

#### Aluminosilicates (Zeolites)
- Si replaced by Al in framework
- Large cavities for ion exchange
- Applications: water softening, catalysis

### Germanium, Tin, Lead

| Element | Key Facts |
|---------|-----------|
| Ge | Semiconductor; transparent to IR |
| Sn | Two allotropes: α-Sn (gray, diamond), β-Sn (white, metallic); transition at 13.2°C |
| Pb | Soft, dense metal; +2 preferred over +4 |

**Dichloride stability:** CCl₂ < SiCl₂ < GeCl₂ < SnCl₂ < PbCl₂

## Group 15: The Nitrogen Family

### Elements
N, P (nonmetals); As, Sb (metalloids); Bi (metal)

### Key Properties

| Element | Atomic # | Valence Config | χ | Common Oxidations |
|---------|----------|----------------|---|-------------------|
| N | 7 | 2s²2p³ | 3.0 | -3 to +5 |
| P | 15 | 3s²3p³ | 2.1 | -3, +3, +5 |
| As | 33 | 4s²4p³ | 2.0 | -3, +3, +5 |
| Sb | 51 | 5s²5p³ | 1.9 | +3, +5 |
| Bi | 83 | 6s²6p³ | 1.9 | **+3** |

### Inert-Pair Effect
**+5 oxidation state stability decreases** from P to Bi:
- Bi(V) is a strong oxidant
- Bi₂O₅ unstable; Bi₂O₃ stable

### Nitrogen Chemistry
- 9 oxidation states possible
- Strong N≡N triple bond (945 kJ/mol)
- Forms multiple bonds readily

### Phosphorus Chemistry
- P₄ tetrahedra (white P)
- Red P: polymeric
- H₃PO₄ (phosphoric acid) - industrial importance

## Group 16: The Oxygen Family

### Elements
O, S, Se, Te (nonmetals/metalloids); Po (metal, radioactive)

### Key Properties

| Element | Atomic # | Valence Config | χ | Common Oxidations |
|---------|----------|----------------|---|-------------------|
| O | 8 | 2s²2p⁴ | 3.5 | -2, -1 |
| S | 16 | 3s²3p⁴ | 2.5 | -2, +4, +6 |
| Se | 34 | 4s²4p⁴ | 2.4 | -2, +4, +6 |
| Te | 52 | 5s²5p⁴ | 2.1 | +4, +6 |
| Po | 84 | 6s²6p⁴ | 2.0 | +2, +4 |

### Key Trends
- **No stable metals** in this group
- **Catenation decreases** down group
- **O²⁻ stability decreases** down group (Po doesn't form Po²⁻)
- **Oxygen forms multiple bonds**; S, Se prefer chains/rings

### Oxygen Compounds

| Type | Formula | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Oxides | MO, MO₂ | Basic (metals), acidic (nonmetals) |
| Peroxides | H₂O₂, Na₂O₂ | O₂²⁻ ion |
| Superoxides | KO₂ | O₂⁻ ion |

### Sulfur Compounds

| Compound | Uses |
|----------|------|
| H₂SO₄ | Strongest industrial acid |
| SO₂, SO₃ | Acid rain precursors |
| S₈ rings | Elemental sulfur allotrope |

## Hydrogen: Unique Element

### Position
- **Not in any group**
- Can form H⁺ (like Group 1) or H⁻ (like Group 17)
- Forms covalent bonds (like Group 14)

### Isotopes

| Isotope | Symbol | Mass | Uses |
|---------|--------|------|------|
| Protium | ¹H | 1.0078 | Most common |
| Deuterium | ²H (D) | 2.014 | Heavy water, tracers |
| Tritium | ³H (T) | 3.016 | Radioactive tracer |

## Decision Flow

### Predicting Oxidation State
1. **Group 13**: +3 (Tl prefers +1)
2. **Group 14**: +4 (Pb prefers +2)
3. **Group 15**: +5 or +3 (Bi prefers +3)
4. **Group 16**: +6, +4, -2 (heavier prefer +4)

### Predicting Bond Type
1. **C, N, O**: Strong multiple bonds (good π overlap)
2. **Si, P, S**: Weaker multiple bonds (poor π overlap)
3. **Heavier elements**: Prefer single bonds, chains

### Predicting Oxide Type
1. **Nonmetal oxides**: Acidic
2. **Metal oxides**: Basic
3. **Near diagonal**: Amphoteric (Al₂O₃, ZnO, SnO₂)
