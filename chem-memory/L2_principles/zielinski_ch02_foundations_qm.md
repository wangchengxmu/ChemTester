# Foundations of Quantum Mechanics — Zielinski Ch2

**Source:** Quantum States of Atoms and Molecules, Ch2

## Key Concepts

Four key experiments that led to quantum mechanics:
1. **Black-body radiation** (Planck, 1900)
2. **Photoelectric effect** (Einstein, 1905)
3. **Compton effect** (1923)
4. **Hydrogen luminescence spectrum** (Balmer, Rydberg)

### 2.2 Black-Body Radiation
- Wien's law: λ_max = hc/(5k_BT) (displacement law)
- Rayleigh-Jeans: ultraviolet catastrophe
- Planck: E_n = nhν — energy quantization

### 2.3 Photoelectric Effect
- KE = hν − φ (φ = work function)
- No emission below threshold frequency ν₀ = φ/h
- Intensity affects number of electrons, not their KE

### 2.4 Compton Effect
- Δλ = (h/m_e c)(1 − cos θ) — wavelength shift from x-ray scattering
- Confirms particle nature of photons: p = h/λ

### 2.5 Hydrogen Luminescence
- Balmer series (visible): 1/λ = R_H(1/4 − 1/n²)
- Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R_H(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)
- R_H = 109,677 cm⁻¹

### 2.6–2.8 Bohr Model
- Electron orbits with quantized angular momentum: L = nħ
- Energy: E_n = −13.6 eV/n² (hydrogen)
- **Rydberg equation derivation**: E_n = −μe⁴Z²/(2ℏ²n²) → Rydberg constant

### 2.9 Wave Properties of Matter
- **de Broglie**: λ = h/p
- Standing wave condition explains Bohr quantization
- Electron diffraction confirms wave nature
