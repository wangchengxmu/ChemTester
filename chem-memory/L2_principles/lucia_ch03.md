# Hazards in Green Chemistry

## Source
[Key Elements of Green Chemistry (Lucia), Chapter 3](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Environmental_Chemistry/Key_Elements_of_Green_Chemistry_(Lucia)/03%3A_Hazards)
Author: Lucian Lucia, North Carolina State University

## Key Concepts

### Hazard Definition and Classification
- **Hazard**: inherent property of a substance with potential to cause harm
- **Risk** = Hazard × Exposure (control exposure to reduce risk)
- Physical hazards: flammability, explosivity, reactivity
- Health hazards: acute toxicity, chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, mutagenicity
- Environmental hazards: persistence, bioaccumulation, toxicity to aquatic organisms

### Hazard Assessment Tools
- **GHS (Globally Harmonized System)**: standardized hazard classification and labeling
- **Safety Data Sheets (SDS)**: 16-section documents with hazard information
- **R-phrases and S-phrases** (European, being replaced by GHS)
- **Inherent Safety Principles**: minimize, substitute, moderate, simplify (ISMS)

### Case Study: Badger Army Ammunition Plant
- 7,400-acre contaminated military site in Wisconsin
- Contaminated with carbon tetrachloride, trichloroethylene, dinitrotoluenes
- Cleanup cost > $250 million
- Illustrates long-term environmental and economic costs of hazardous processes

### Green Technologies for Safer Chemical Production
- Process intensification: doing more with less equipment/energy
- Flow chemistry: continuous processes vs. batch (safer, more efficient)
- Microwave and ultrasound-assisted reactions
- Supercritical CO₂ as reaction medium
- Biocatalysis: enzymes replace hazardous reagents

## Formulas
- Risk = Hazard × Exposure
- NFPA diamond ratings: Health (blue), Flammability (red), Reactivity (yellow), Special (white)
