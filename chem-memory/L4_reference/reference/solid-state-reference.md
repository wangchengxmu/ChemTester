## Classification of Solids

| Type | Components | Bonding | Examples | Typical MP |
|------|------------|---------|----------|------------|
| Ionic | Cations + Anions | Electrostatic | NaCl, CaO, Al₂O₃ | 400-3000°C |
| Molecular | Molecules | IMFs | Ice, I₂, Sugar | -200 to 200°C |
| Covalent Network | Atoms | Covalent bonds | Diamond, Si, SiO₂ | 1000-4000°C |
| Metallic | Metal atoms | Metallic | Fe, Cu, Al | -39 to 3422°C |

## Key Formulas

### Lattice Energy (Born-Landé)
```
U = (NA × M × z⁺ × z⁻ × e²)/(4πε₀ × r₀) × (1 - 1/n)
```
- M = Madelung constant
- n = Born exponent (typically 5-12)

### Lattice Energy (Kapustinskii)
```
U = 120200 × z⁺ × z⁻ × (1 - 34.5/r₀)/r₀
```
- r₀ in pm
- U in kJ/mol

### Madelung Constants
| Structure | Madelung Constant |
|-----------|-------------------|
| NaCl | 1.748 |
| CsCl | 1.763 |
| ZnS (zinc blende) | 1.638 |
| CaF₂ (fluorite) | 2.519 |

## Properties by Bond Type

### Ionic Solids
- High melting points
- Hard but brittle
- Poor solid conductors
- Good conductors when molten
- Often water-soluble
- Transparent to visible light

### Molecular Solids
- Low melting points
- Soft
- Poor conductors
- Often volatile
- Variable solubility
- Often transparent

### Covalent Network Solids
- Very high melting points
- Very hard (except graphite)
- Poor conductors (except graphite)
- Chemically inert
- Insoluble in most solvents
- Often transparent

### Metallic Solids
- Variable melting points
- Malleable and ductile
- Excellent conductors
- Lustrous
- Insoluble in water
- Opaque
