# Cannizzaro eligibility with competing carbonyl pathways

## Applicability

Counting or classifying aldehydes that can undergo Cannizzaro chemistry, especially α-branched or multifunctional carbonyl structures.

## Procedure

1. Inventory every aldehyde and every other enolizable carbonyl; count α-hydrogens at each site and identify possible enolate donors and aldehyde acceptors.
2. Treat an aldehyde with no α-hydrogen as an ordinary Cannizzaro candidate; direct attachment of the formyl carbon to an aromatic ring normally satisfies this screen.
3. For an aldehyde with alpha hydrogens, assess aldol and other competing pathways. A tandem aldol/Cannizzaro sequence is a condition- and substrate-specific possibility, not automatic eligibility; require an identified supporting reaction and conditions.
4. For multifunctional molecules, enumerate intramolecular enolate attacks and favorable ring closures, but do not declare a dominant pathway from ring size alone without kinetic or condition-specific support.
5. Assign one verdict per compound after resolving all sites and competing pathways, then map the resulting count to the requested answer format.

## Boundaries

- Do not treat exactly one α-hydrogen as unconditional Cannizzaro eligibility.
- Do not inspect only the aldehyde α-carbon in a polycarbonyl molecule; audit every enolizable site and feasible ring closure.
- Do not let broad “can undergo” wording license hypothetical trace or nonstandard pathways when an established competing reaction controls the classification.
- Count compounds rather than aldehyde groups unless the prompt explicitly asks for functional-group counts.
