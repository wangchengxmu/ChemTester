# Diels-Alder bridged-product face and descriptor audit

## Applicability

A Diels-Alder product choice requires connectivity, endo or exo assignment, or named and R/S-defined stereochemistry for a bridged adduct, including ordinary cyclopentadiene with a monosubstituted electron-poor alkene.

## Procedure

1. Map diene and dienophile atoms into the completed product skeleton, preserve the diene internal bond and dienophile relationships, and number the actual bridged framework before assigning stereochemistry; a group directed toward the highest-numbered bridge is exo and one directed away is endo, while any separate diene-face preference remains an independent axis.
2. Draw one internally consistent three-dimensional adduct, then assign priorities and R/S independently at each listed center by following complete ring paths on the final graph rather than translating an endo label into a memorized descriptor tuple.
3. As a checked calibration for an otherwise unsubstituted norborn-5-ene-2-carboxylate alkyl ester, the endo pair is (1R,2R,4R)/(1S,2S,4S), whereas the exo pair is (1R,2S,4R)/(1S,2R,4S); changing the ester O-alkyl group does not change the ring-center assignments.
4. Recognize the enantiomeric partner expected from achiral reactants and distinguish global inversion from a single-center mismatch.
5. Compare each option as a full feature vector of connectivity, bridge orientation, retained relationships, endo or exo geometry, and every descriptor before selecting it.

## Boundaries

- Do not translate endo or exo directly into a memorized R/S tuple; for the conventional norborn-5-ene-2-carboxylate calibration, do not swap the endo and exo C2 descriptor while keeping the same bridgehead enantiomer.
- Do not equate endo or exo with a separate syn or anti facial preference.
- Recompute priorities on the product graph because the two ring paths can rank differently than visual intuition suggests.
- Check global inversion before treating two absolute descriptor sets as different constitutional or relative-stereochemical products.
