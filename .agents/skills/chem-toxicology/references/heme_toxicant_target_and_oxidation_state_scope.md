# Heme toxicant target and oxidation-state scope

## Applicability

A qualitative chemistry or toxicology question compares ligand binding, oxidation, denaturation, or no interaction across hemoglobin states and other hemoprotein targets.

## Procedure

1. Separate the prompt’s exact target claim from the substance’s dominant whole-body toxicity mechanism.
2. Distinguish functional ferrous hemoglobin, ferric methemoglobin, and ferric heme centers in respiratory enzymes before judging binding or redox claims.
3. For cyanide, recognize coordination to ferric heme: it binds methemoglobin and inhibits cytochrome oxidase, while binding normal ferrous hemoglobin poorly; binding is not denaturation or oxidation.
4. Evaluate each visible option at its stated scope: reject an absolute no-interaction claim when a documented heme-state interaction exists, then choose the least overbroad true statement.
5. Return the requested option format before explanation.

## Boundaries

- Do not use the dominant toxic target to negate a real secondary blood interaction.
- Do not collapse functional hemoglobin and methemoglobin into the same iron oxidation state.
- Do not equate ligand binding with oxidation, denaturation, or the primary cause of toxicity.
- Do not add a deterministic tool for a qualitative reference-fact distinction.
