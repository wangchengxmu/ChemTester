# Compound-specific toxicity within broad chemical classes

## Applicability

A qualitative toxicology question compares broad chemical families or asks whether every member of a class shares one toxicity level.

## Procedure

1. Identify the classification level of each claim: exact compound, narrow homologous series, functional subclass, or broad chemical family.
2. At broad-family level, audit whether structure, substitution, oxidation state or speciation, physical properties, dose, and exposure route can materially change toxicokinetics or toxicodynamics.
3. Reject a universal class ranking unless the question supplies a restricted scope or reliable evidence that supports it; a memorable hazardous member is not representative evidence for the whole family.
4. Evaluate each answer choice at the same stated level of generality, distinguishing intrinsic hazard from exposure-dependent risk, before selecting the best-supported claim.

## Boundaries

- A shared functional-group label does not imply uniform toxicity.
- Do not infer a family-wide hazard from one well-known toxic member.
- Keep intrinsic toxicity separate from exposure probability and risk.
- Empty retrieval is not evidence for a remembered class-wide rule; preserve uncertainty instead.
