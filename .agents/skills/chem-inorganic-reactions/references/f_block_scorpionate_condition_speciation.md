# F-block scorpionate condition and coordination audit

## Applicability

An f-block coordination problem combines an element-identification clue, a poly(pyrazolyl)borate reagent, reaction conditions, and a requested metal, oxidation state, ligand set, or coordination number.

## Procedure

1. Enumerate element candidates from every naming and physical-property clue; for density anomalies record allotrope, temperature, pressure, and source precision before excluding candidates.
2. Preserve the precursor metal and oxidation state unless an explicit redox or transmetalation step changes them, then build a ligand charge and donor-count ledger.
3. Treat hydrotris(pyrazolyl)borate as monoanionic and usually tridentate. Require matched structural or reaction evidence for B-N cleavage, azolate formation or altered denticity; dryness or crowding alone does not establish a decomposition product.
4. Expand omitted ligand multiplicities and verify coordination number by summing multiplicity times denticity; use charge neutrality as an independent check.
5. Select a catch-all choice only after every explicit composition has failed the identity, condition, charge, and coordination audits.

## Boundaries

- Astronomical naming is not a unique element identifier; reconcile it with the full constraint set.
- Do not infer an unlisted metal from one clue and stop before auditing the synthetic evidence.
- Count donor atoms, not ligand names; multiplicity and binding mode are part of the formula.
- Do not invoke a hydrolysis-derived ligand under explicitly dry conditions without a stated oxygen source.
