# Matched acid and counteranion trend audit

## Applicability

A matched substituent series compares both Brønsted acidity and coordination or ion-pairing of the corresponding conjugate anions, especially with highly electrophilic cations and weakly coordinating cluster anions.

## Procedure

1. Write the neutral-acid and conjugate-anion series separately, and identify which property each claim ranks.
2. Compare acid and conjugate-base strengths in the same medium and standard-state convention; do not substitute gas-phase proton affinity for solution acidity without a solvation model.
3. Treat weaker coordination by the conjugate base of a stronger acid as a conditional hypothesis, not a ranking rule. Fix the cation, solvent, phase and temperature, and assess polarizability, softness, sterics and charge exposure using direct evidence.
4. Cross-check interaction strength with association energies, cation distortion, vibrational shifts, or other direct observables before completing the statement truth set.

## Boundaries

- Neither copying nor reversing an acidity order alone establishes anion coordination; leave the coordination order unresolved when interaction-specific evidence is missing.
- Do not compare raw contact distances across different atom sizes as a standalone measure of interaction strength.
- Do not assume a weakly coordinating anion is noninteracting with a highly electrophilic cation.
- Do not transfer solid-state or solvent-specific ion-pair trends across unmatched conditions.
